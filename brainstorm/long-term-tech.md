# Long-term tech (10–100 years)

Tim (2026-10-27): "work on thinking about things that might take more years to develop (10-100 years)."

These are on the website's Ideas tab in the **11–20**, **21–50** and **50+ year** groups, with what each needs first.

## What really limits the speed
1. **People, not ideas.** Every engineer has to be raised and taught. One generation of trained people takes ~20 years; the second generation, taught by the first, is when things explode.
2. **Precision and materials.** Each new machine needs tighter tolerances and purer metals than the last.
3. **Scale.** A lab demo isn't a railway. Mines, factories and supply chains take decades.

## Real-world speed checks
- Britain, inventing as it went: steam engine (1712) → railways (1830) → electricity grid (1880s) = **~170 years**.
- **Japan, copying known tech: 1868 → beat Russia in 1905 = 37 years.**
- South Korea: farming country (1960) → electronics leader (2000) = **40 years**.
- Our hero knows *what works* but has no blueprints or factories to copy. A fair guess: he compresses **~250 years of real history (1700–1950) into ~80–120 years.** He won't live to see the end: someone must carry it on (a story idea).

## Roughly in order
### 10–20 years
- **Watt steam engine** and **steam factories**
- **Machine-made cloth** (spinning and weaving machines)
- **Bessemer steel**, **canals and locks**, **railways**, **steamships**
- **Electric telegraph network**, **photography**, **X-rays**
- **City water and sewer systems**

### 20–50 years
- **Electric grid and light bulbs** (carbon filament + good vacuum + dynamo)
- **Telephone** (the carbon microphone is surprisingly simple)
- **Oil refining** (kerosene), **refrigeration** (frozen food shipped around the world)
- **Breech-loading rifles and cartridges**, **smokeless powder**, **machine guns**
- **Synthetic dyes** from coal tar → **sulfa drugs** (the first antibiotics, easier than penicillin)
- **Internal combustion engine**, **steel-hull ships**, **radio**
- **Haber-Bosch fertilizer**, **aluminum**

### 50–100 years
- **Cars, trucks and tractors**, **airplanes** (the barrier is a light, powerful engine)
- **Plastics** (Bakelite: phenol + formaldehyde)
- **Mass-produced penicillin**
- **Vacuum tubes → electronic computers**
- **Transistors** (needs ultra-pure germanium or silicon)
- **Nuclear power** (needs a whole advanced industry + uranium)
