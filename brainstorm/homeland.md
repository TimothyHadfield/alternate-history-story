# Where to build — the hidden homeland question

Tim (2026-10-27): "I'm thinking it might be smartest to start this society somewhere that is kind of hidden and protected from powerful nations so that they don't get demolished due to their growing power and so that the other civilizations don't steal inventions and use them against this civilization. I'm thinking maybe the Americas could be good, maybe Indonesia, parts of Africa, Australia, etc? ... Do you think that would be smartest or no? ... Resource density is also something to consider. Maybe the benefits of trade don't compensate the risk of war or military conflict with a greedy civilization."

## Short answer
**Hidden is smart, but *fully* isolated is a trap.** The best plan is a **"walled garden with one gate"**: a protected, resource-rich home far from the big powers, plus **one controlled trade port** that sells finished goods and buys what the home lacks, while never letting the know-how out.

## Why hiding helps
- **Not being crushed early.** In 1230 the Mongols are flattening everyone from China to Hungary. A small growing kingdom on their path dies before it's strong. This is the biggest reason, bigger than tech theft.
- **Time.** Europeans don't reach the Americas, southern Africa or Australia for 260–540 years. That's a guaranteed head start.
- **Freedom to lead.** No pope, emperor or neighbor king to answer to; it's easier to become the core leader of a smaller society.

## Why full isolation hurts
- **Industry needs people.** Britain had ~6–9 million people when it industrialized; factories need workers *and* customers. Most isolated places have very few people in 1230.
- **Missing basics.** The Americas and Australia have **no horses, oxen or cattle, no wheat, no iron smelting**. You'd be bootstrapping from further back.
- **Disease time bomb.** Isolated peoples have no immunity to Old World diseases. When outsiders finally arrive, up to 90% can die (this is what destroyed the Aztecs and Inca more than guns). The hero can fix this with vaccination, but **vaccines need cattle (cowpox)**. In the Americas he'd have to sail to the Old World and bring vaccine back **arm-to-arm** in a chain of people (Spain really did this with orphans in 1803).
- **No trade = slower wealth.** Trade brings metals you lack, animals, crops, skilled immigrants and news.

## Is tech theft really the danger?
Less than it seems. **Simple ideas** (crop rotation, gunpowder recipes, handwashing) leak fast and can't be protected. **Deep tech** (steam engines, steel, electricity) **can't be copied without the whole industrial base**: a 1300s king who captures a rifle still can't make one. History shows secrets *can* be kept a long time:
- China kept **porcelain** secret ~1,000 years.
- Venice forbade its **glassmakers** to leave Murano, on pain of death.
- Arab traders hid where **cinnamon** came from with myths of giant birds for centuries; the source of **nutmeg** (the tiny Banda Islands) stayed hidden from Europe until 1512.

So the real rules are: **sell products, never processes**; keep weapons and machine tools at home; and **don't look rich until you're strong.**

## Does trade pay for the risk of war?
- **Early (years 0–30): yes, through middlemen.** Sell gold, glass, steel blades, spirits and dyes through existing traders who don't know where they come from. Low visibility, high profit.
- **Middle (30–80): carefully.** One official port, foreign ships allowed only there (Japan's Dejima and China's Canton worked this way for centuries).
- **Late (80+): openly**, once your navy and guns make attacking you unthinkable. Then you can trade with everyone and control the sea lanes.
- History warning: wealthy but weak places get eaten (the Inca by Spain, the Swahili coast by Portugal, the Banda Islands by the Dutch). **Strength first, visible wealth second.**

### Notes on the top options
- **Southern Africa highveld:** arguably the most mineral-rich land on Earth: the world's biggest gold field (Witwatersrand), huge shallow coal (Witbank), iron (Sishen), copper and iron already mined at **Phalaborwa since ~700 AD**, tin mined at **Rooiberg**, diamonds, platinum. In the 1220s the **Mapungubwe kingdom** (on the Limpopo) has a king, iron-working farmers, cattle (= oxen and cowpox) and already sells gold to the Indian Ocean through Sofala and Kilwa: **a ready-made trade gate.** Far from the Mongols; the Portuguese don't round the Cape until 1488. Problems: malaria in the river valleys (the high plateau is healthy), droughts, few navigable rivers, small population. It also sits next to the **Cape of Good Hope**, the future sea route between Europe and Asia.
- **Japan:** ready-made skilled, organized population (~6 million), excellent smiths, silver and copper, island defense. It's real-world proof: Japan sealed itself off and then industrialized faster than anyone (1868–1905). Weaker in iron ore and coal. Built-in test: the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281.
- **North America:** the 1900s industrial heartland: Pittsburgh coal and iron, Lake Superior's pure copper and Mesabi iron, oil, the Mississippi river network, the best farmland on Earth. Total isolation for centuries. But you start with no iron, no draft animals, no wheat, and a thin population (Cahokia is declining).
- **Andes:** silver (Potosí), tin, copper, gold, mercury, the world's best fertilizer (guano islands and Atacama nitrates). Organized people with roads and terraces. But mountains make transport brutal, there's little coal, and no strong draft animals (llamas carry ~30 kg).
- **Australia / New Zealand:** maximum safety and huge resources, but almost no farmers. Best as a **second homeland** later: a fallback fortress the outside world doesn't know exists.

## Claude's recommendation
**Southern Africa highveld, starting at the Mapungubwe kingdom (~1220s).** It's the best balance: hidden for ~260 years, absurdly resource-dense, an existing kingdom with iron, cattle and a gold trade route to sell through, and it's almost unused in fiction. Runners-up: **Japan** (best people, less hidden) and **North America** (best resources and isolation, slowest start).

*This is Tim's call. Changing the location also means the "1230 Europe" assumptions in the time estimates shift a bit.*
