# Rising to power — how a modern person gets to the top

Tim (2026-10-27): "start exploring ways of how a modern day person might grow to power in different societies and civilizations within this time period, assuming they are able to know practically anything the modern day knows? this might just be demonstrating proofs about mathematics, developing technologies and gaining influence, predicting events, etc. I don't love the predicting events thing; it's kind of a cliche, the people would think I'm more of a god than anything else and it's not repeatable ... Maybe if I revealed to a single powerful person I'm from the future and then predicted an event to prove it to get them on my side, but besides that it's not my favorite."

## The core rule: repeatable proof beats prophecy
A prophecy works once and makes you a god or a witch. **A repeatable demonstration makes you an expert people can rely on**, and they can check it themselves. Good proofs are:
- **Repeatable:** works every time, in front of anyone.
- **Useful to the powerful person:** saves their money, their army, or their child.
- **Explainable:** "here's why it works", so it's knowledge, not magic.

**Tim's one exception:** reveal the truth to **one** powerful patron and use **one** prediction to prove it. That gives him a partner who understands, without the public treating him as a god.

## The first year (any society)
1. **Survive:** language, food, not being enslaved or killed as a stranger. (He knows nobody, has no status, no money, maybe no language.)
2. **Be useful in a small, repeatable way** to someone with a little power (a merchant, a village head, a lord's steward).
3. **Climb to a patron** with real power.
4. **Build his own wealth and people**, so he isn't just a servant.
5. **Get his own base**: land, a workshop town, followers.

## Ten routes to power
| Route | How it works | Example proof | Speed | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Healer** | Save the ruler's family or army | Oral rehydration saves a dying child in a day; clean childbirth; setting fractures; boiled-water rule ends camp dysentery | Fast | If a patient dies anyway |
| **2. Treasurer** | Fix the ruler's money | Arabic numerals + double-entry bookkeeping exposes theft and finds hidden income within weeks | Fast | Makes enemies of corrupt officials |
| **3. Military engineer** | Every ruler pays for war | Better trebuchet range by math, field fortifications, camp hygiene, crossbow drill, later gunpowder | Medium | Being captured or used by rivals |
| **4. Merchant** | Get rich, then buy influence (the Medici way) | Sell spirits, soap, mirrors, dyes; find loose gold or silver where he knows it is | Medium | Robbery, guilds, taxes |
| **5. Scholar** | Fame through brilliance; courts collect geniuses | Math proofs, solving famous problems, computing tables, building instruments | Slow but prestigious | Rivals, envy |
| **6. Farmer-reformer** | Feed a lord's lands better than anyone | Crop rotation with clover, better plow and harness, seed selection. Visible in one harvest | 1–2 years | Slow, weather |
| **7. Founder** | Lead refugees or settlers to new land and become their founder | Organize, feed and protect a fleeing group (e.g. people fleeing the Mongols) | Medium | Hardship, raiders |
| **8. Warlord** | Train a small, disciplined, healthy force and win | Drill, logistics, medicine, communications | Fast in chaos | Very high |
| **9. Marriage / alliance** | Marry into a ruling family | Becomes trusted kin | Medium | Court politics |
| **10. Secret partner** (Tim's idea) | Reveal the truth to one ruler, prove it once, then co-rule from behind the throne | One prediction + a stream of useful knowledge | Fast | Patron dies or betrays him |

Most real stories combine 2–3 routes. A strong arc: **Healer or Treasurer → Secret partner → Founder of his own state.**

## Proofs that impress without looking like magic
- **Math:** the Pythagorean theorem proved several ways, π to many digits, compound interest, predicting a cannonball's or trebuchet stone's landing, instant multiplication with logarithm tables.
- **Medicine:** oral rehydration, boiled water, clean wound care, a homemade magnifying lens showing "tiny creatures" in pond water.
- **Engineering:** a pulley system lifting a huge weight with one person, a pump, a better horse harness, a water-powered hammer.
- **Chemistry:** making soap, distilling strong spirits, a compass needle magnetized from a lodestone, invisible ink.
- **Practical tricks:** a sundial or calendar more accurate than the court's; a map; measuring a tower's height from its shadow; finding north at night.

## How it differs by society (1230s)
| Society | How power works | Best routes for an outsider |
|---|---|---|
| **European kingdom** | Birth, land, the court, the Church | Healer, Treasurer, Military engineer, Scholar. **Emperor Frederick II** literally held math contests: Fibonacci solved problems posed at his court in 1225 |
| **Mamluk Egypt / Islamic sultanates** | Merit counts: Mamluks were slave-soldiers who could become sultan. Doctors and scholars highly honored | Healer, Scholar, Military engineer |
| **Mongol Empire** | Ruthless but merit-based; hired foreign experts (Persian siege engineers took Xiangyang in 1273; Marco Polo served Kublai Khan) | The fastest route to huge power, but he'd be serving the empire everyone fears |
| **Song China** | Civil service exams, scholars rule | Hard for a foreigner; Merchant or Engineer through a local official |
| **Japan** | Samurai loyalty to lords | Become a valued retainer: smithing, medicine, military engineering |
| **Mapungubwe (southern Africa)** | King and elite own cattle and control the gold trade | Healer (people *and* cattle), rainy-season farming and water (wells, irrigation), metalwork, trade goods for the elite, marriage alliance |
| **Americas chiefdoms** | Chiefs, kinship, prestige | The tech gap is largest, so any route works fast: metal tools, medicine, farming |

## Protecting himself once he's useful
- **Never write down the full recipe.** Key knowledge stays in his head or is split between people who don't know each other's piece.
- **Be more valuable alive and free** than captured: keep producing new things.
- **Build his own followers** loyal to him, not to the patron (students, healed families, freed captives).
- **Avoid looking like a god.** Teach openly; "anyone can learn this" makes him a teacher, not a threat to the gods or the king.
