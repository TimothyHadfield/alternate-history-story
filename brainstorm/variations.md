# Six variations: same hero, six different worlds

*For comparison. None of these is chosen.*

Tim (2026-10-28): "Pick the top options and then design a full-out plan for each one as if we are writting a book where each location is a different variation. I want each option to be different enough that they each are unique and have their own strategies, tasks, and meathods of growing. I'm thinking maybe 5-7 different locations. Can you pick them for me?"

## The six picks
Six of the top 10 were picked, chosen for score **and** for being different from each other. **Each one uses a different route to power and a different way of growing.**

| # | Variation | Overall /10 | Route to power | How the group grows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | [**North America: the Atlantic Exodus**](variations/north-america.md) | 7.8 | **Founder.** He rises at Frederick II's court, then leads refugees across the Atlantic | **Settlers plus local peoples**, Old World animals and crops on the best land |
| ② | [**Southern Africa: the Rainmaker's Kingdom**](variations/southern-africa.md) | 6.1 | **Partner to a sacred king**, through water, cattle and healing | **Drawing in neighboring peoples** to an isolated, mineral-rich plateau |
| ③ | [**Britain: the Merchant Inside**](variations/britain.md) | 6.2 | **No throne.** Money, a company, Oxford, charters | **Economic growth** inside a competitive Europe: an early industrial revolution |
| ④ | [**Java: the Spice-Sea Empire**](variations/java.md) | 6.1 | **Court favorite** in a new dynasty where an outlaw just became king | **A maritime trade empire**: rice, ships and spices, later Australia |
| ⑤ | [**Andes: the Organizer**](variations/andes.md) | 5.7 | **Unifier**, through storehouses and organized labor, 200 years before the Inca | **A planned command economy** built from scratch with no outside help |
| ⑥ | [**Sweden: the Man Behind the Jarl**](variations/sweden.md) | 5.7 | **Secret partner** to Birger Jarl, who rules Sweden from 1248 | **A small, hardy, cold-land state**: iron, timber and immigration |

## Why these six
- **They span every way of rising:** founding, marrying in or advising a king, getting rich, serving at court, unifying, and the secret-partner route.
- **Each has a different main constraint:**
  - ① distance
  - ② water
  - ③ rivals who copy everything
  - ④ disasters and disease
  - ⑤ total isolation
  - ⑥ cold
- **Contact with the Old World runs from none to total:**
  - ⑤ Andes: none
  - ① North America: only what he brings
  - ② Southern Africa: one gate (Sofala)
  - ⑥ Sweden: the Baltic
  - ④ Java: a crossroads
  - ③ Britain: in the middle of everything

## Left out, and why
- ~~Japan~~ (5.5): the hardest place to rise in (2/10). It also overlaps with Java (Asian islands with volcanoes) and Britain (a crowded island state).
- ~~Central Chile~~ (5.0): no central ruler to win, and it overlaps with the Andes.
- ~~Ethiopia~~ (4.8): hard terrain and transport, and power is guarded by the Church and nobles.
- ~~SE Australia~~ (4.5): nothing to farm at first and no people to lead. It appears as a later frontier for Java.
