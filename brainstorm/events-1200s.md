# Major events of the 1200s–1300s (around the world)

Tim (2026-10-27): "explore specific major things that are happening during this time period."

Each event is marked as a **threat** (could destroy him), an **opportunity**, or a **clock** (a known date he can plan around or "prophesy").

## The Mongols — the century's main event (threat)
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1206 | Genghis Khan unites the Mongol tribes |
| 1211–1234 | Conquest of northern China (the Jin) |
| 1219–1221 | Destroys the Khwarezm Empire (Central Asia, Persia); cities like Samarkand and Merv massacred |
| 1227 | Genghis dies |
| 1237–1240 | Conquest of the Russian principalities; Kyiv destroyed |
| 1241 | Crushes Polish and Hungarian armies, then turns back when the Great Khan dies |
| 1258 | Destroys Baghdad, the heart of Islamic science |
| 1260 | First real defeat: the Egyptian Mamluks win at **Ain Jalut** |
| 1274 & 1281 | Invasions of Japan wrecked by typhoons ("kamikaze") |
| 1279 | Finish conquering Song China |
| 1293 | A Mongol fleet invades Java and is driven out |

The Mongols never reached southern Africa, the Americas or Australia, and they failed against Japan and Egypt.

## Middle East and North Africa
- **1250:** the **Mamluks** (slave-soldiers) take over Egypt; they beat the Mongols in 1260. Cairo becomes the Islamic world's main city.
- Islamic science peaks then suffers after **Baghdad falls (1258)**. Scholars flee: possible recruits.

## Asia
- **China:** the Song dynasty (south) is the richest, most advanced state on Earth until the Mongols take it (1279).
- **Japan:** the Kamakura shogunate (samurai government). Survives two Mongol invasions.
- **India:** the **Delhi Sultanate** fights off repeated Mongol raids. In the south, rich Hindu kingdoms (Pandya, Chola) trade with Southeast Asia and Arabia.
- **Southeast Asia:** **Angkor** (Cambodia) at its peak; **Srivijaya** (Sumatra) declining; **Singhasari** (Java, 1222) → **Majapahit** (1293), which will rule much of Indonesia.
- **Korea:** invaded by the Mongols from 1231; casts metal movable type (1234).

## Africa
- **Mali Empire** founded (~1235) by **Sundiata**; controls West Africa's gold. Timbuktu becomes a center of learning.
- **Mapungubwe** (~1220–1290): the first southern African kingdom, rich in gold and cattle; then **Great Zimbabwe** (~1290–1450).
- **Swahili coast** (Kilwa, Mombasa): wealthy trading cities linking Africa to Arabia, India and China.
- **Ethiopia:** the Zagwe kings carve the rock churches of **Lalibela**; Solomonic dynasty from 1270.

## Americas
- **Cahokia** (near St. Louis), the largest city north of Mexico (~10–20k people at its peak), is **declining** after 1200.
- **Mexico:** the Toltecs have fallen; the **Mexica (Aztecs)** arrive in the Valley of Mexico ~1250 and found Tenochtitlan in 1325.
- **Andes:** the **Chimú** kingdom rules the Peruvian coast; the **Inca** are a small kingdom around Cusco (empire only after 1438).
- **Maya:** Mayapán rules the Yucatán.
- **Ancestral Puebloans** abandon Mesa Verde during the great drought (~1276–1299).

## Oceania
- **New Zealand is uninhabited** until the Māori arrive ~1250–1300.
- Polynesian voyagers have settled most Pacific islands; **Australia** has Aboriginal peoples, no farming states.

## The planet
- **Medieval Warm Period ending → Little Ice Age** from ~1300: colder winters, failed harvests.
- **1257 Samalas eruption** (Indonesia): one of the largest in 2,000 years; cold years follow worldwide.
- **Norse Greenland** colony slowly dying (gone by ~1450).

## The windows these events open
- **Who to avoid:** anywhere on the Mongol path (China, Central Asia, Persia, Russia, Eastern Europe) between 1210 and 1300.
- **Who to recruit:** refugee scholars from Baghdad and Khwarezm; craftsmen from fallen cities.
- **Who could be a patron or trade partner:** Frederick II, Venice, the Mamluks, the Swahili cities, Mali.
- **Free "prophecies":** 1241, 1257, 1258, 1260, 1274, 1281, 1315, 1347.
