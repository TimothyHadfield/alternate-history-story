# Time periods — what each era had, and how good a landing spot it is

Goal (Tim): "a time where the inventions/processes are relatively simple but put the people extremely far ahead of the rest of the world."

The sweet spot is a society that **already has iron, literacy, money and a strong state** (so it can build what he describes), but **not yet the scientific revolution** (so the leap is enormous). Too early and there's nothing to build with; too late and the gap is small.

| Era | Dates | What they already had | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Bronze Age (Egypt, Sumer) | 3000–1200 BC | Writing, bronze, irrigation, pyramids | Too primitive: no iron, tiny literate class. Fun "god-king" story but slow to industrialize. |
| Classical Greece | 500–300 BC | Geometry, philosophy, early physics, democracy | Brilliant thinkers, but small fractured city-states. |
| **Late Roman Republic / Early Empire** | 100 BC–200 AD | Concrete, roads, aqueducts, glassblowing, Hero's toy steam engine (aeolipile), huge state, literacy | **Strong pick.** Massive resources and organization. Problem: slavery makes labor-saving machines seem pointless (a great story theme though). |
| Late Antiquity / Byzantium | 400–600 AD | Roman engineering surviving in the East | Good, but this is exactly what *Lest Darkness Fall* did. |
| Early Middle Ages | 500–1000 | Heavy plow, horse collar, stirrups spread | Weak states, low literacy. Harder. |
| Song Dynasty China | 960–1279 | Gunpowder, printing, compass, paper money, huge iron output (rivaled 1700s Britain) | The most advanced society on Earth — arguably closest to an industrial revolution. Less "far ahead" contrast. |
| Islamic Golden Age | 800–1250 | Algebra, optics, chemistry, hospitals, Arabic numerals | Great science base; Baghdad falls to the Mongols in 1258. |
| **High Middle Ages (Europe)** | 1100–1300 | Watermills and windmills everywhere, iron, cathedrals, first universities, Venetian glass, banks, guilds; spectacles appear ~1286 | **Strong pick.** Classic "medieval" feel Tim described. Mongols invade 1241 — a built-in deadline. |
| Late Middle Ages | 1300–1450 | Gunpowder cannon, mechanical clocks; Black Death 1347 kills ~1/3 of Europe | Plague is a perfect early test of germ theory and quarantine. |
| Renaissance / Age of Exploration | 1450–1650 | Printing press, ocean ships, the Americas | Gap is getting smaller; the real scientific revolution is about to start anyway. |
| Early Modern | 1650–1750 | Newton, Newcomen engine | Too late — they're almost there already. |

## Recommendation
**High Middle Ages Europe, around 1200–1230.** Why:
- It's the "medieval" world Tim pictured, with castles, kings, the Church and knights.
- They already have the *raw materials of industry*: iron, waterpower, coal (mined in England), glassmakers, banking, universities. They can actually build Tier 1–2 things.
- But they're ~500 years before the steam engine. The gap is enormous.
- **Built-in threat and clock:** the Mongol invasion of Europe (1241) and the Black Death (1347). It fits the "develop our technology as fast as they can so they don't get destroyed" idea even inside the past.
- Location options: **England** (coal + iron + island defense, the real birthplace of industry), **Venice** (glass, ships, banking, trade), **Poland/Hungary** (front line vs. the Mongols), **Sicily** under Frederick II (a curious, science-loving emperor).

**Runner-up: Late Roman Republic (~70–50 BC).** Bigger state, better engineering, and Julius Caesar as a character. Downside: slavery and Roman politics are brutal, and the "medieval" feel is gone.

*This is Tim's call — nothing is locked.*
