# Dates, and the series idea

*Exploring possibilities. Nothing here is decided.*

Tim (2026-10-27): "The date clash is extreamly close together. do you think we should pick a different date? I also had an idea where maybe we could make a series of stories where people go back in time but to different time periods and whatnot."

## The clash
*The Cross-Time Engineer* (1986): an engineer arrives in **1231 Poland** and prepares for the **1241 Mongol invasion**. Our ~1220–1241 window with a Mongol deadline is nearly the same.

**How much it matters:** a shared *year* is fine (hundreds of novels share WWII). A shared *year + enemy + goal* (beat the Mongols) is what reads as a copy. So the choices are:

| Option | What changes | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Keep ~1225, change the story** | Different place, no "defeat the Mongols" plot (e.g., Frederick's court, then elsewhere); the Mongols are background, not the climax | Readers of Frankowski will still notice the year |
| **Move earlier, ~1180–1200** | 40–60 years before the Mongols. Young Fibonacci (Liber Abaci comes out in 1202, so the hero could shape it). The Third Crusade (1189) and Saladin are on stage | Less pressure from a fixed deadline |
| **Move to ~1320, Black Death deadline (1347)** | A health story: germ theory, quarantine, rat and flea control, clean water. Fits "simple ideas with massive implications" better than any war | Different research (plague, 1300s Europe) |
| **A different era entirely** | e.g., late Roman Republic (the earlier runner-up), Song China, Bronze Age (~1200 BC) | Most of the 1230 research would need redoing |

**Claude's view:** the **Black Death (arrive ~1320)** is the strongest swap. It has a hard deadline like the Mongols, it's the biggest killer in history, and the weapons against it are hygiene and knowledge, not guns. That makes it clearly *not* Frankowski. Second choice: **~1190–1200**, the same world but earlier, with Fibonacci still unknown.

## The series idea
**Several stories, each a different person sent to a different era.** It fits the parked framing idea well: a dystopian future that sends **several travelers**, each to a different turning point.

Possible structures:
- **Anthology:** each book stands alone with its own traveler, era and approach (one with a scientist, one a doctor, one a soldier, one an economist).
- **Same program, different missions:** the future runs one program; each book is one mission. Later books can show earlier missions' effects, or failures.
- **Converging:** the travelers' changes start overlapping and they find out about each other.

Turning points with built-in deadlines, one per book:

| Era | Deadline / catastrophe |
|---|---|
| ~1200 BC | Bronze Age collapse (~1177 BC) |
| ~50 BC | Late Roman Republic, before the civil wars |
| ~520 AD | Plague of Justinian (541) |
| ~1225 | Mongol invasions (1241) |
| ~1320 | Black Death (1347) |
| ~1480 | Contact between the Old and New Worlds (1492); the epidemics that follow |

A series also takes the pressure off the date clash: the Mongol era can be one book among many, told differently.
