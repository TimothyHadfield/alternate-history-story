# Tech tree — what an older society could build, and how fast

Organized by **how much infrastructure it needs**, not by date. The story's best moments live in Tier 0: things that need almost nothing but change everything (Tim: "the best improvements are simple things that nobody thought about but have massive implications").

Legend: ⭐ = huge impact for very little effort.

---

## Tier 0 — Ideas (need nothing but authority and teaching)

### Health (the fastest way to win loyalty: fewer kids die)
- ⭐ **Germ theory + handwashing + boiling water.** Semmelweis cut maternal deaths in his clinic from ~18% to ~2% with chlorinated handwashing (1847).
- ⭐ **Oral rehydration salts** — clean water + a pinch of salt + a fistful of sugar/honey. Dysentery and cholera stop killing most of their victims. Often called the most important medical advance of the 20th century.
- ⭐ **Latrines away from wells**, quarantine, burying the dead away from water. (John Snow's cholera pump, 1854.)
- ⭐ **Smallpox: cowpox vaccination** (Jenner, 1796). Needs only a milkmaid's cow and a needle.
- **Scurvy:** citrus/sauerkraut on ships. **Rickets:** sunlight/fish oil. **Goiter:** seaweed/iodine.
- **Alcohol as antiseptic** (distill wine), sterilize tools in fire/boiling water, clean childbirth.
- **Triage, field medicine, tourniquets, splinting** — a huge edge in war.

### Food (more food = more people = more workers/soldiers)
- ⭐ **Four-field crop rotation** with clover/legumes (fixes nitrogen) — the Norfolk system roughly doubled yields in 1700s Britain.
- **Seed drill, selective breeding, manure/composting, drainage, iron plows, scythes over sickles.**
- **New World crops** (potato, maize) — only if he can reach the Americas. The potato alone may have driven a quarter of Old World population growth after 1700.
- **Canning** (sealed jars + boiling, Appert 1809) — armies and sailors eat year-round.

### Math and thinking
- ⭐ **Hindu-Arabic numerals + zero + decimals.** Roman numerals make arithmetic miserable.
- ⭐ **Double-entry bookkeeping** — this is what let Venice and Florence run empires of trade.
- **The scientific method itself** — test, measure, write it down, repeat. Arguably the biggest one.
- Algebra, trigonometry (navigation, surveying, artillery), probability/statistics (insurance, census), **calculus** (needed later for engineering and orbits — cool but not the first payoff).
- **Standard weights and measures** (a metric system) — required before anything can be mass-produced.

### Organization and economics
- **Division of labor / assembly lines, interchangeable parts** (Venice's Arsenal built a ship a day in the 1500s).
- **Patents, joint-stock companies, insurance, banks, paper money backed by reserves.**
- **Public schooling + literacy**, a postal system, a census, a professional army with drill and logistics.

## Tier 1 — Craft level (blacksmiths, potters, glassblowers can do it with direction)
- ⭐ **Printing press** (movable metal type, oil-based ink, screw press). Every other item on this list spreads 100× faster.
- ⭐ **Lenses → spectacles → telescope → microscope.** The microscope *proves* germ theory (people can see the "animalcules"). The telescope wins naval and land battles.
- **Soap** (lye from wood ash + fat). **Paper** (rags/wood pulp).
- **Distillation** (alcohol, essential oils, acids).
- **Gunpowder** (saltpeter from manure/urine heaps + charcoal + sulfur), **rifled barrels, minié ball.**
- ⭐ **Semaphore/optical telegraph** (towers + telescopes + a code book; Chappe, 1790s). Messages travel across a country in hours. No electricity needed.
- ⭐ **Hot-air balloon** (Montgolfier, 1783): paper/silk + fire. Aerial scouting — terrifying to a medieval army.
- **Lightning rods**, chimneys, efficient stoves, glass windows, mirrors.
- **Pendulum clock**, magnetic compass, sextant, accurate maps, longitude by chronometer.
- **Better ships:** sternpost rudder, lateen + square rig, hull shapes, copper sheathing.
- **Roman-style concrete** (lime + volcanic ash/pozzolana) — harbors, roads, aqueducts.
- Wheelbarrow, horse collar, stirrups (period-dependent: some already exist).

## Tier 2 — Early industrial (needs iron, coal, and a machine shop)
- ⭐ **Blast furnace + coke from coal** (Darby, 1709) → cheap cast iron.
- ⭐ **Bessemer process** (1856): blow air through molten iron → cheap steel. Surprisingly simple concept.
- **Screw-cutting lathe** (Maudslay, ~1800) — the "mother machine". Precision is THE bottleneck of the whole tech tree.
- ⭐ **Steam engine:** Newcomen (1712) is crude and tolerant of bad machining — perfect first engine for pumping water out of mines. Then Watt's separate condenser (1769) for 3–4× efficiency. Then high-pressure engines → locomotives and steamships.
- **Railways** (start with wooden rails + horses, like real mining wagonways).
- **Chemistry industry:** sulfuric acid (lead chamber), soda ash, bleach, fertilizers (bone meal, guano, saltpeter).
- ⭐ **Anesthesia — ether** (alcohol + sulfuric acid, distilled). 1840s, but only needs Tier 1 chemistry. Painless surgery.

### Electricity (Tim's field — EE)
- ⭐ **Voltaic pile** (1800): stack of copper and zinc discs with brine-soaked cloth between. Absurdly simple; could have been built by the Romans.
- **Electromagnet** (1820s) → **electric telegraph** (1830s–40s): wire + battery + electromagnet + Morse code. Instant long-distance communication.
- **Electroplating, electrolysis** (isolate new elements, purify copper).
- **Dynamo / generator** (Faraday's induction, 1831) — copper wire spun near magnets. Needs lots of wire.
- **Arc lamp** (bright light for lighthouses/forts).
- Bottlenecks: **drawing long copper wire** and **insulating it** (silk, shellac, waxed cloth, gutta-percha).

## Tier 3 — Needs an industrial base first (the story's "endgame")
- Crystal / spark-gap radio (actually simple once wire + batteries exist).
- Internal combustion, oil refining, rubber (needs tropical plants).
- Penicillin (mold is easy, mass-producing it is hard), broad vaccines.
- Haber-Bosch fertilizer (high pressure — very hard), electric grid, steel warships, rifles with cartridges, machine guns.

## The real bottlenecks (good story conflict)
1. **Precision.** Knowing what a steam engine looks like doesn't let you bore a cylinder true. Watt's first engines failed until Wilkinson's cannon-boring mill (1774).
2. **Material quality:** steel, clear glass, pure chemicals.
3. **Measurement:** no standard units, no thermometer, no accurate clock.
4. **Resources:** coal, iron, copper, tin, sulfur, saltpeter. He needs to know *where* they are (geology knowledge is a superpower).
5. **People:** skilled craftsmen, literacy, training enough workers.
