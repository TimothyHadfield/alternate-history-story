# North America: the Atlantic Exodus

*He climbs at the cleverest court in Europe, then leads a fleet of refugees, cattle and craftsmen to found a new state on the best rivers on Earth.*

*This is one variation, written for comparison with the others. It is not a decision.*

## What makes this one different
- **Two stages.** He rises in Europe first (Frederick II's court and Pisa), then leaves. Every other variation grows where he lands. This one **moves the whole project across an ocean.**
- **He's the founder, not a climber.** In stage 2 there is no king to win or throne to stand behind. He arrives as the head of his own people, so his power is **built in from day one**.
- **Growth by carrying, then joining.** The start is a **fleet**: people, animals, seed, tools and skills packed into ships. After that, the group grows by **joining with local peoples** (Fort Ancient, Monongahela, Iroquoian villages and Cahokia's Mississippian chiefdoms), who gain the animals, metal and vaccines.
- **Stopping a catastrophe before it starts.** Historically, Old World diseases killed most Native Americans. Here he can make **smallpox vaccination** part of the founding, using **cowpox** carried by arm-to-arm chain, then grown in calves.
- **The richest land, the longest supply line.** Coal in the riverbanks, iron, surface copper, salt springs, the best farmland and **the best water power on Earth**. There's no going back for parts, though: what isn't in the fleet must be made locally.

## The setup
**Arrival:** spring **1230**, at **Pisa**, in Tuscany. He has nothing but his clothes and what's in his head: an EE student's physics, chemistry, math, medicine basics and a rough sense of history and geography.

**The world around him:**
- **Frederick II** (Holy Roman Emperor, King of Sicily) has just come back from his crusade (1229). He's curious, sceptical and loves experiments. He collects scholars like **Michael Scot** and tests people with math puzzles.
- **Leonardo of Pisa (Fibonacci)** is in Pisa. He revised his *Liber Abaci* in **1228**. In **1240** the city gives him a yearly salary for advising on accounts.
- **Pisa** is a major sea power, trading with Sardinia, Sicily, Tunis and the Levant. Its shipyards and merchants are what stage 2 needs.
- **To the east, the Mongols** are coming. Kiev falls in 1240. Hungary is crushed at **Mohi in April 1241**.
- **Across the ocean, no one in Europe knows.** The Norse in Greenland remember "Vinland", but nobody else does. The interior of North America has never seen iron tools, wheels, horses or cattle.

**The language:** he must learn **Tuscan Italian** first, then **Latin** for the court. A modern speaker of English with some Spanish or French could get by in Italian within a year *(reasoned)*. In stage 2 he faces Hungarian, Cuman (Turkic), Slavic tongues and several Native language families (Iroquoian, Algonquian, Siouan, Muskogean).

**The people he will lead** don't exist as a group yet. He has to gather them.

## The rise to power
### Stage 1: the court expert (1230–1242)
| Years | Step | Proof or tool | Who he wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1230–31 | Survives as a **bookkeeper and scribe** among Pisan merchants. Learns the language | **Double-entry bookkeeping** and fast Hindu-Arabic arithmetic, already fashionable thanks to Fibonacci | A merchant house that hires him |
| 1231–33 | Meets **Fibonacci**, trades math with him: decimal fractions, algebra notation, interest tables | Fibonacci becomes a friend and **the door to the court** | Fibonacci |
| 1232–35 | Makes money from **simple things nobody thought of**: **spectacles** (historically invented near Pisa ~1286), **distilled spirits** as medicine, **hard soap**, clear glass | Each one is repeatable and sells | Pisan glassmakers and investors |
| 1234–36 | Presented to **Frederick II**. Solves his puzzles, then proves himself as a **healer** (boiling water, clean childbirth, rehydration salts) and **engineer** (water mills, surveying) | Fewer deaths in his household; a working mill | Frederick and his officials |
| ~1236 | **The one secret.** He tells Frederick the truth and gives **one proof**: a prediction only Frederick will see | Tim's single-prediction exception | Frederick, privately |
| 1236–40 | Frederick funds a **"western voyage" in secret**. One ship, a trusted Pisan captain and the hero's maps go via the Canaries and the trade winds | The scout ship comes back in **1240** with maize, tobacco and a sketched coastline | Captains and pilots |
| 1239–42 | Buys ships, recruits **craftsmen**, hunts for **cowpox** in dairy country | Stocks seed, iron, tools and books | Smiths, millwrights, masons, shipwrights, midwives |
| 1241–42 | **The Mongols strike Hungary.** King Béla IV flees to the Dalmatian coast (Trogir, Split). He shelters refugees there | Food, medicine, safe passage | **Hungarian farming families, Cuman herders, Rus' and Polish refugees** |

**Why Frederick lets him go:** Frederick can't hold Italy *and* a new world. The hero offers him a **share and a secret**: a far land that sends back things of value, and an ally beyond the reach of every rival. *(This is the main risk. A patron who won't let his expert leave is the classic trap.)*

**Risks in stage 1:** being seen as a sorcerer, rivals at court, Frederick's quarrels with other powers dragging him into politics, and the money running out before the fleet is ready.

### Stage 2: the founding (1242 onward)
- **Autumn 1242:** the fleet gathers on the Adriatic, sails to **Gibraltar** and then the **Canaries**.
- **December 1242:** it crosses on the **trade winds** (Columbus's route, about 5 weeks), avoiding hurricane season.
- **Early 1243:** it rests at **uninhabited Bermuda** or a quiet Caribbean cay (water and quarantine), then rides the **Gulf Stream** north along the coast. The Jamestown fleet of 1607 took the same Canaries and West Indies route.
- **April 1243:** it lands in **the Hudson River** and sails upriver to near modern **Albany**, where ocean ships stop. *(The Hudson is chosen over the St. Lawrence because it's ice-free sooner and the river is navigable far inland. The St. Lawrence is the alternative.)*

**The moment he holds real power:** the day the **founding charter** is signed on board, before landfall. Every family signs or marks it. It sets out who decides what, how land is shared and how the founder is succeeded. From then on, his authority comes from his people's agreement, not from any European crown.

## How the group grows
### Where the people come from
| Source | When | Rough numbers | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The first fleet** | 1243 | **~3,000** | About 20 ships. Craftsmen plus refugee farming and herding families |
| **Births** | Always | **3%+ a year** | Abundant land and food, plus soap, clean water and midwife training. Families of 6–8 surviving children were normal in colonial America |
| **Return fleets** | Yearly from 1245 | 500–2,000 a year | Via the secret gate. Recruited from the poor, refugees and famine victims |
| **Famine waves** | 1258, 1315–17 | Thousands | Europe starves while this land has a surplus |
| **Native towns joining** | From 1245 | The **largest pool** by far | Whole villages join the federation for vaccines, iron, animals and stored food |
| **After the Black Death** | 1350s | Careful, small | Only after long quarantine (see Hazards) |

### Target numbers *(reasoned guesses)*
- **1250:** 6,000 settlers, plus ~10,000 in allied towns
- **1270:** 25,000 settlers, plus ~80,000 allied
- **1300:** ~100,000 core, plus ~400,000 in the federation
- **1350:** ~1 million in all
- **1400:** 2–3 million

Tim's idea, "maximum population growth as well as maximum immigration while maintaining positive growth", fits this variation better than most: there's endless room, and the limit is **food, housing and health keeping up**.

### Keeping up
- **Food:** every new family gets **land, seed and loaned oxen** in year one. Granaries hold **two years** of grain as a buffer.
- **Housing:** water-powered **sawmills** from year 1 mean plank houses instead of huts. Brick and lime kilns come by year 3.
- **Health:** every newcomer is **vaccinated and quarantined** at the port before going inland. Towns are laid out with **latrines downstream of the wells**.

## Government and society
**The settler core:** towns elect their own councils. The **founder** holds a few powers for life: the ports and the gate, the schools, the vaccine, the land registry and the final say on the tech plan. Everything else is local.

**Joining with local peoples:** this is **a federation, not a conquest**.
- A town joins by agreement, keeps its own chiefs and customs, and sends speakers to a **federation council**.
- The deal it gets: **vaccination**, **iron tools**, **breeding animals** (a pair of oxen, piglets, hens), **seed wheat**, the right to trade at federation markets, and a share in stored grain during droughts.
- Land is **never taken**. Settlers farm land granted by agreement, at first in thinly peopled areas.
- This echoes the real **Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) League**, a working federation of nations. Its founding date is debated (some say 1142, others ~1450) *(uncertain)*.

**Fitting the culture:**
- The **Mississippian** towns (Cahokia, Angel Mounds, Kincaid) have hereditary chiefs and mound-top temples. The founder approaches them as **an equal chief from far away** who brings gifts and knowledge.
- **Iroquoian** villages have strong **clan mothers** who decide on land and choose chiefs. Their women farmers are the first to want ox-plows and new seed.
- **Gift-giving and feasts** are how alliances work here, so iron and livestock come as gifts first and are traded after.

**Language:** in this variation the working language is the craftsmen's **Italian**, written in a simple **phonetic alphabet** he designs. Every school is **bilingual** (Italian plus the town's own language).

**Schools:**
- From year 1, every child aged 6–12 learns reading, numbers and hygiene.
- Apprenticeships with the craftsmen.
- A **founder's school** of about 30 picked students (settler and Native) who learn everything: medicine, chemistry, machines, surveying. They are the future leaders and engineers.

**Succession:** the leader isn't expected to die for a long time but plans for it anyway:
- A **sealed "if I die" book** of instructions and knowledge, updated every year. Copies are kept at two separate sites.
- A **named heir** from the founder's school, confirmed by the council. The heir doesn't have to be his own child.
- Everything critical (the vaccine method, the gate, iron-making) is known by **at least three people**.

## The tech path
Local resources and where they are:
- **Coal:** the Pittsburgh seam, outcropping on the hills above the Forks of the Ohio.
- **Iron:** bog ore everywhere; carbonate and limonite ore in western Pennsylvania and the Hanging Rock region of Ohio.
- **Copper:** pure native copper on Michigan's Keweenaw Peninsula and Isle Royale (Lake Superior).
- **Lead:** galena in the Upper Mississippi lead district (Galena, Illinois, and southwest Wisconsin).
- **Salt:** springs at Onondaga (Syracuse), the Kanawha (West Virginia) and Big Bone Lick (Kentucky).
- **Oil:** seeps at Oil Creek (Titusville, Pennsylvania).
- **Saltpeter:** cave earth in Kentucky (Mammoth Cave was mined for it in 1812).
- **Flint:** Flint Ridge, Ohio.
- **Water power:** Cohoes Falls (where the Mohawk meets the Hudson), Genesee Falls (Rochester), Niagara, Cuyahoga Falls and the Falls of the Ohio (Louisville).
- **Timber:** oak, chestnut, white pine for masts, and hickory and maple.

| Phase | Key tasks and inventions | Why this order *here* |
|---|---|---|
| **Years 0–2** (1243–45) | Quarantine camp. **Wells and latrines.** Plant wheat, oats, flax and turnips beside Native maize, beans and squash. Water-powered **sawmill and gristmill** at Cohoes Falls (millwrights and iron fittings came in the fleet). Smithy using carried iron. **Charcoal**, **potash** from cleared wood ash, soap. **Vaccine calf farm.** Meet neighbors | Shelter, food and health before anything else. The fleet's iron is **finite**, so it goes only into mills and plows |

Clover rotation. Scouting to Lake Ontario and Lake Erie. River **flatboats** | Making iron locally is the turning point: until then every broken tool is a loss. Salt preserves meat and keeps cattle healthy |

Fertilizer. Universities | The rivers are already the highways, so steamboats come before railways. Niagara is one of the greatest power sites in the world |

## Food, water and health
The first tasks, in order:
1. **Quarantine before landfall.** No smallpox or measles case in the fleet for **3 weeks** before anyone goes ashore near Native people. The ocean crossing itself acts as the quarantine.
2. **Vaccinate everyone.** In stage 1 he hunts for **cowpox** in the dairy country of Lombardy and Hungary, offering a reward for "pocks on cows' udders or milkmaids' hands". *(Cowpox in cattle is sporadic, so this may take years. The backups are horsepox and, at worst, careful variolation.)* On the voyage, the vaccine stays alive **arm to arm** through a chain of unvaccinated volunteers, one new carrier every 9 days. This is the method of the Balmis expedition, which carried smallpox vaccine to the Americas in 1803 with 22 orphan boys.
3. **The calf farm.** Once landed, the vaccine is grown on the bellies of **calves** (the Italian method of the 1840s), so it never depends on a human chain again.
4. **Vaccinate the neighbors as a gift.** "The scar that protects" becomes the first thing every Native town wants. **Long-term payoff:** when Columbus-era ships arrive in the 1490s, the federation is already **immune to smallpox**, the disease that historically did the most killing.
5. **Wells and latrines.** Wells uphill, latrines downhill, and **boiling water** for babies and the sick.
6. **Oral rehydration salts** (clean water, salt, honey or maple sugar) for dysentery.
7. **Soap** from potash and animal fat, which they have plenty of.
8. **Malaria screening.** Malaria didn't exist in the Americas before Europeans. **Don't recruit from malarial marshes** (the Maremma, the Pontine Marshes), and quarantine anyone with recurring fevers. Without this, the Ohio Valley gets the "ague" that plagued its later settlers.
9. **Rats and fleas.** Cats on every ship, and food stored in rat-proof bins. There is no plague in Europe in the 1240s, but there will be in the 1340s.
10. **Food crops together:** the **Three Sisters** (maize, beans and squash, which the neighbors already grow) beside **wheat, rye, oats and barley**, with **red clover and turnips** in rotation, which feeds cattle through winter. **Maple sugar** in spring. **Orchards** from grafted apple and pear cuttings brought in wet moss.
11. **Animals to carry** *(rough; water on board is the limit, since a horse drinks 30–40 liters a day)*: about **150 horses, 300 cattle, 400 sheep and goats, 200 pigs**, plus chickens, geese, **honeybees** in skeps, cats and dogs. Pigs breed fastest and forage in the woods.
12. **The prairie sod.** The deep prairie further west needs a **steel moldboard plow** (John Deere's 1837 idea). It's a simple thing with a huge effect, and it comes once there's steel.

## Trade and the outside world
**The gate:** **one Pisan merchant house**, partners from stage 1, plus **a secret waystation in the uninhabited Azores**. Portugal won't find the Azores until the 1420s. Ships coming home ride the **westerlies** straight past the Azores, so the waystation sits on the natural route.

| Goes out | Comes in |
|---|---|
| **Beaver and other furs**, which are hugely valuable in Europe | **People** (the main import for 50 years) |
| **Spectacles**, mirrors and glass | **Breeding animals** and new seed varieties |
| Distilled spirits and maple sugar | **Books** and manuscripts |
| Later: steel tools, paper, printed books | **Tin** (eastern North America has almost none, and it's needed for bronze and solder) |
| | Mercury, alum, dyes and silk, at first |

**Secrets:**
- **The route is the secret.** Ideas like spectacles, numerals and soap are **allowed to spread** in Europe, because they can't lead anyone to the land.
- **Pilots and maps** stay with the federation's own ships after year 15.
- Europe hears only that Pisa has **a good supplier of furs** "from the north".
- There's no neighbor with ships or steel until about 1500, so **neighbor danger is about 9/10**.

## Story texture
**Tensions:**
- **Founder and partners.** He wants to *join* with Native peoples, but some settlers want to *take* land. The charter is tested early.
- **Leaving Frederick.** A patron who loves him and doesn't want to lose his best mind.
- **Fibonacci stays behind.** The friend who won't cross the ocean. Do they write letters through the gate?
- **Cumans and Hungarians on the same ships.** Hungarian nobles murdered the Cuman khan in 1241, and both peoples are now refugees together.
- **The vaccine scar.** At first it's feared, then it becomes a badge everyone wants.
- **Mixed children.** A generation that is neither European nor Native, but the federation's own.

**Real people:**
- **Frederick II** (the secret)
- **Fibonacci** (the friend)
- **Michael Scot** (the court scholar, who dies ~1235)
- **King Béla IV** in Trogir (gratitude, and maybe refugees sent willingly)
- In America, the names are unrecorded: invented chiefs and clan mothers at Cahokia, Monongahela and Iroquoian villages.

**Scenes:**
- The **puzzle duel** at court, with Fibonacci watching.
- **The scout ship comes back** in 1240 with a basket of maize.
- **The arm-to-arm chain:** a child's arm checked on day 9 in a storm.
- **Landfall:** cattle swimming ashore in the Hudson.
- **The first ox pulling a plow** in an Iroquoian field while the clan mothers watch.
- **The first iron poured** at the Forks of the Ohio, glowing beside the coal seam.
- **Arriving at Cahokia's Monks Mound** by river, with gifts.
- **The 1348 message from Pisa:** "Europe is dying. Do not come."
- **A telegraph line along the Ohio** carrying news that once took a month.

## Strengths and weaknesses
**Ratings from [location-factors.md](../location-factors.md):**

| Measure | Score |
|---|---|
| Land (water 10, climate 6, disasters 6, food 7, imports 3, health 8, energy 10, transport 10, building 8, room 10) | **7.8** |
| Ease of rising to power (with settlers) | **8** |
| Neighbor danger (10 = safest) | **~9** |
| Culture acceptance | **6** (8 with the people he brings) |
| **Overall (North America with settlers)** | **7.8**, the highest on the list |

**Strengths:**
- The **best resources and water power on Earth**, close together and linked by rivers.
- **No throne to climb.** His power is secure from 1243.
- **Total isolation** for ~250 years, so ideas aren't copied and there's no arms race.
- **Endless room** for maximum growth and immigration.
- The **smallpox rescue** is a hugely positive, story-defining achievement.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Everything rides on stage 1.** If Frederick won't release him or the fleet fails, there is no stage 2.
- **The voyage.** Animals die at sea, ships get lost. It's a one-shot bet.
- **A small start** (~3,000), and a **long supply line** for tin, books and specialists.
- **Disease is a double-edged sword.** One mistake (measles, flu, malaria) could cause the very catastrophe he's trying to prevent.
- **Many peoples and languages**, with no single ruler to win, so joining is slow, town by town.
- **Cold winters** and a 150–180 day growing season in the north.
