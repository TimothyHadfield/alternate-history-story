# Java: the Spice-Sea Empire

*A navigator at the world's busiest crossroads turns a young Javanese kingdom into a sea-trading empire, then uses its ships to open Australia's iron and coal.*

**This is one variation, written for comparison. It is not a decision.**

---

## What makes this one different
- **The sea is the country.** The other five grow on land (a new state, a plateau, an island economy, the Andes, a cold kingdom). This one grows **by ships**: rice feeds sailors, sailors carry trade, and trade pays for everything else.
- **Foreigners are normal here.** Chinese, Tamil, Gujarati, Persian, Malay and Khmer merchants already live in the ports. A strange man with strange skills is **just one more stranger**, so he can start fast. The flip side: **ideas leak fast too**, and he has to choose which ones to let go.
- **Millions of skilled people from day one.** Java already has writing, markets, money, roads, irrigation and **2–3 rice harvests a year**. He doesn't build a society. He **redirects a rich one**.
- **Two homes, far apart.** Java has people and food but little iron and coal. **Northern Australia** has the iron (the Pilbara) and the coal and room, but almost no people. The long game is a **sea bridge** between them.
- **The hazards are natural and huge.** A super-eruption (**Samalas, 1257**), constant volcanoes and **tropical disease**, plus **one large fleet (1293)**. Health and disaster readiness are a bigger share of the work here than anywhere else.

---

## The setup
**Arrival:** about **1235**, at the port of **Hujung Galuh** (near modern Surabaya), at the mouth of the **Brantas River** in East Java. *(The exact site of Hujung Galuh is debated.)*

**The kingdom:** **Singhasari**, then called **Tumapel**, with its court near modern **Malang**, up the Brantas valley in the highlands. It is only 13 years old. Its founder, **Ken Arok**, was a commoner and outlaw who killed his way to the throne and **beat the old kingdom of Kediri in 1222**.

**The succession he walks into** (dates are uncertain; the two chronicles disagree by a year or more):

| King | Reign (main dates) | How it ended |
|---|---|---|
| **Ken Arok** (Rajasa) | 1222–1227 | Killed by his stepson Anusapati |
| **Anusapati** | 1227–1248 | Killed by his half-brother Tohjaya, at a cockfight |
| **Tohjaya** | 1248 (months) | Overthrown by his nephews |
| **Wisnuwardhana** (Ranggawuni) | 1248–1268 | Died naturally; named his son king in **1254** to secure the throne |
| **Kertanegara** | 1268–1292 | Killed by a rebel vassal, **Jayakatwang of Kediri** |

*(Pararaton-based dates run 1248–1250 for Tohjaya and 1250–1272 for Wisnuwardhana. This file uses the Wikipedia dates.)*

So in 1235 the king is **Anusapati**, a man who killed his stepfather and **lives in fear of being killed himself**. The two boys who matter for the future, **Ranggawuni** (later Wisnuwardhana) and his cousin **Mahisa Cempaka**, are children or teenagers.

**The world around him:**
- **China:** the **Southern Song** dynasty, rich and sea-trading. The Mongols have just taken northern China (1234). Chinese junks already use the **compass**.
- **Sumatra:** **Srivijaya** is fading. Its old port role is up for grabs.
- **The Spice Islands (Moluccas):** **Ternate and Tidore** grow cloves; the tiny **Banda Islands** grow all the world's nutmeg and mace. Javanese ships already carry these west.
- **India and the Arab world:** they buy spices, sell cloth, glass and horses.

**What he has:** nothing. No tools, no money, no books. Only what an Electrical Engineering student knows: **math, physics, basic chemistry, how things are built, and a good feel for geography and modern history.**

**The language:** **Old Javanese** (Kawi) at court; Malay is the trade language of every port. He learns Malay first (it's simple, and every trader speaks some), then Javanese. Writing already exists (Kawi script on palm leaf), so **he can write his notes in Malay or Javanese within two or three years.**

## The rise to power
The route is **useful stranger → port expert → royal tutor → chief minister**. He never takes the throne. Javanese kings are semi-sacred, and a foreigner on the throne would split the court. Real power comes from **being the one the young king trusts**.

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **1235** | Arrives with nothing. Works on the docks and ships of Hujung Galuh. Learns Malay. Fixes things: a better pump for bilge water, a better pulley rig for unloading. |
| **1236–37** | Shows sailors **how to find latitude** from the noon sun and the stars with a simple quadrant he makes. Draws a **map of the islands to scale** that's better than anything the ship captains own. A Chinese captain and a Javanese harbor master take notice. |
| **1238** | The **harbor master** of the port hires him to count cargo and tax. He introduces **Arabic numerals and double-entry books**. Tax income from the port jumps, because less is stolen. *First proof:* money the court can see. |
| **1239–40** | Sent up the Brantas to the court. Grows a test field with **transplanting in straight rows, spacing and drying the field mid-season** (modern rice techniques). Yields rise by a third. *Second proof:* food. |
| **1241** | Becomes **tutor to the young prince Ranggawuni** and his cousin Mahisa Cempaka. Teaches them math, maps and how the world is shaped. This is the key relationship. |
| **~1244** | **The one secret proof.** He tells **only Ranggawuni** who and what he is, and backs it with one checkable fact: a **solar or lunar eclipse** on a stated date. Nobody else ever hears it. |
| **1248** | **The crisis.** Tohjaya kills Anusapati and moves to kill the two princes. The hero has quietly prepared: a hidden route, trusted guards, a boat. The princes survive and **overthrow Tohjaya** (as in real history), but with his planning. |
| **1248** | **Real power.** Wisnuwardhana makes him **chief minister of ports, ships and works** (a new office). Mahisa Cempaka is co-ruler, as he was in real history. |
| **1254** | Wisnuwardhana names his young son **Kertanegara** king-in-waiting. The hero becomes **his** tutor too. Now he has shaped two generations of rulers. |

**Who he has to win:**
- **Ranggawuni / Wisnuwardhana:** the main patron, for life.
- **The harbor masters and big merchants:** the port families who run trade. He makes them richer, so they back him.
- **The Chinese merchant community:** they carry the news, the crafts and the junk-building know-how.
- **Mahisa Cempaka:** the co-ruler. He has to be a friend, not a rival.

**The risks:**
- **Court murder is normal.** Three of the first four kings died by violence. He has to **avoid being anyone's target** and never look like he wants the throne.
- **Being copied.** Foreign traders see everything and sail home.
- **Being just "a clever foreigner."** He must stay useful, not exotic.

## How the group grows
**Starting point:** Singhasari's core (the Brantas valley and Malang highlands) has perhaps **0.5–1 million people**. All of Java may have **3–5 million** *(estimates vary a lot)*.

**The idea Tim raised, applied here:** maximum births plus maximum immigration, while growth stays positive.

| Source | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Births** | Fewer babies dying: clean water, **oral rehydration** (salt and palm sugar), mosquito control, midwife training. Rice surplus means families can afford more children. | From the 1240s |
| **Neighbors joining** | Other East Javanese princes and **Kediri** join peacefully because joining means irrigation, ships and medicine. Later Bali, Madura and the coasts of Borneo and Sumatra. | 1250s–1290s |
| **Trade immigrants** | Every port already has foreign quarters. He **makes settling easy**: land grants for craftsmen, low taxes for the first years, a court that treats all merchants by the same written rules. | Always |
| **Refugees** | **Song Chinese fleeing the Mongols**, especially after the Song fall in **1279**. Tens of thousands of shipwrights, potters, iron-workers, doctors and scholars left China by sea. He sends ships to bring them. | **1270s–1280s** (the biggest wave) |
| **Australia settlers** | Javanese, Chinese and Bugis families paid to settle the north coast of Australia, near the iron and coal. | 1300s on |

**Targets (reasoned guesses):**

| Year | Core population | Whole realm |
|---|---|---|
| 1250 | 1 million | 1.5 million |
| 1300 | 2.5 million | 6–8 million |
| 1350 | 4 million | 12–15 million |
| 1400 | 6 million | 20+ million, plus ~200,000 in Australia |

**How food keeps up:** Java's rice is the engine. Each new irrigated valley adds a lot of food. **Fish ponds** on the coast, **ducks in the rice fields** (they eat pests and give eggs), and **new crops from Asian trade** add more.

**How housing keeps up:** Java builds in **wood and bamboo** on stilts. That's fast, but it burns and rots. He adds **fired brick and tile** (Java already makes brick, as Majapahit later did at Trowulan), **lime mortar**, and raised floors with screens.

**How health keeps up:** see the Food, water and health section. Health is the hard part here.

## Government and society
**The shape:** a **king with a strong chief minister**, which matches Java's own later model (Majapahit's famous minister **Gajah Mada** ran the empire for a king in the 1330s–1360s). The hero's role is a real Javanese pattern, not an import.

**How it's run:**
- **The king** stays sacred and final. The hero never claims the throne.
- **Ministries** for ports, ships, works (irrigation and roads), health and schools. Each has written rules.
- **Village councils** keep running their own water and fields, as they already do. In nearby Bali the **subak** (water-sharing groups) has run irrigation by agreement for centuries. He builds on that.
- **Bad-news reporting:** every harbor master and district head sends a **monthly written report** with numbers (rice stored, ships in, sickness, prices). Reporting bad news early is rewarded, hiding it is punished. Ships make this fast along the coast.
- **One law for all merchants.** Foreigners, Javanese and Chinese pay the same written tariffs. This is what draws trade from every other port.

**Fitting the local culture:**
- Java absorbs outside ideas through the **court**. New things are introduced as **gifts of the king**, not the hero's personal ideas.
- He works with existing faiths and customs and doesn't change them.
- Status ranks stay. He adds **one new rank of "masters"**: craftsmen, navigators and teachers who earn status by skill.

**Schools:**
- **The navigation school** at the port (first and most important): math, astronomy, charts, ship handling.
- **The court school** for officials' sons and daughters (Javanese women ran markets).
- **Village schools** later: reading and Arabic numerals.

**Succession (planned from the start):**
- The **king's line** carries on as normal, with the hero tutoring each heir.
- **His own office** passes to a **council of masters** he trains, not to a single heir. The best of them becomes chief minister.
- A **written book of methods** so the knowledge doesn't depend on him.
- **The danger point:** the real Kertanegara was killed in **1292** by a vassal. The hero would put **two heirs in two places** (the court and a port) so no single strike ends the line.

## The tech path
**Local resources and where they are:**

| Resource | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rice land and water** | Brantas valley, all of East and Central Java | The best in the region |
| **Teak** | Central and East Java (around Rembang, Blora) | Superb ship timber, resists rot and shipworm |
| **Sulfur** | **Ijen crater**, East Java | For sulfuric acid |
| **Saltpeter** | Bat caves in the limestone hills (**Gunung Sewu**, south coast) | For fertilizer and gunpowder (blasting) |
| **Petroleum seeps** | **Bojonegoro / Cepu**, East Java | Lamp oil, pitch for ships |
| **Iron** | **Lake Matano, Luwu (Sulawesi)**, nickel-rich iron, already traded to Java for kris blades | The nearest good iron. **Australia's Pilbara** later |
| **Tin** | **Bangka and Belitung** (off Sumatra), easy stream tin | For bronze and tinplate |
| **Coal** | **Borneo (south and east Kalimantan)**, large and shallow; Ombilin (West Sumatra); later **Australia** | Java itself has almost none |
| **Salt** | Sea salt on the north coast and **Madura** | Plenty |
| **Cotton, indigo, palm sugar, coconuts, bamboo** | Everywhere | Cloth, dye, rehydration, oil, building |
| **Spices** | **Moluccas** (cloves), **Banda** (nutmeg and mace), pepper in Sumatra | The money |

| Phase | Key tasks and inventions | Why this order, here |
|---|---|---|
| **Years 0–2** (1235–37) | **Quadrant** for latitude, **scale maps**, Arabic numerals, **double-entry books**, better pumps and pulleys | He starts at a port with nothing. Navigation and numbers cost nothing to make and **prove value to the people who run the port**. |
| **Years 3–5** (1238–40) | **Rice: row transplanting, spacing, mid-season drying**, seed selection. **Boiling water, rehydration drink, mosquito drains** around the court. **Compost and lime** | Food and health are what the court cares about. Rice gains spread village to village on their own. |
| **Years 6–10** (1241–45) | **Navigation school**. **Magnetic compass** (adopted from the Chinese junks). **Charts on a grid** (latitude lines, later Mercator). **Ship design**: Javanese jong hull plus **watertight bulkheads** and a **stern rudder** from the Chinese junk. **Paper** (from mulberry or bamboo, with Chinese help) | Ships carry everything here, so ships come before industry. Paper is needed for charts, schools and records. |
| **Years 11–20** (1246–55) | **Dry dock** at the port. **Water wheels** on hill streams (Java has steep rivers off the volcanoes). **Better iron**: buy Luwu ore and bloom, build a **blast furnace** with charcoal. **Brick and lime kilns**. **Mosquito nets** from fine cotton. **Wormwood (Artemisia annua) for malaria**, cold-steeped, as the Chinese text of **Ge Hong** (about 340 AD) describes. **Grain stores for 1257** | Iron and power come once there's food and money to spare. The 1257 eruption sets a hard deadline for **grain storage**. |
| **Years 21–50** (1256–85) | **Longitude**: a good pendulum clock on land, and **lunar distance tables** for ships (he knows the method; the tables take years of observation). **Sulfuric acid** (Ijen sulfur). **Glass and lenses**: spectacles, a **telescope** for the navigation school. **Printing** with movable type. **Coal from Borneo**. **Cannon-free blasting powder** for mining and canals. **First steam pump** (Newcomen-type) at a mine. **Scout voyages to northern Australia** | Clocks and tables need a workshop culture and astronomers, which take 20 years to build. Steam needs iron and coal, which need ships first. |
| **Years 51–100** (1286–1335) | **Iron works on the Pilbara coast** (Hamersley ore, near modern Port Hedland), fed by coal shipped from Borneo or later Australian coal. **Steam engines on ships**. **Cement**. **Electric telegraph** between the ports (copper, zinc and acid batteries). **Vaccination** (cowpox from cattle). **Big canals and harbor works** | By now there are enough trained masters and enough ships to run a second home 1,500 km away. Steam ships make the Java–Australia bridge cheap. |

**The simple things with big effects** (Tim's favorite kind):
- **Latitude by the noon sun.** A stick, a scale, and a table. Every captain gets safer and faster.
- **Scale maps with a grid.** The whole sea becomes plannable.
- **Bulkheads in the hull.** One hole no longer sinks a ship.
- **Salt and sugar in boiled water.** It saves children dying of diarrhea, the biggest killer.
- **Draining standing water.** It cuts malaria and dengue with nothing but shovels.
- **Double-entry books.** The port stops leaking money.

## Food, water and health
Java's food is already the best on the list. **Health is the weak point** (scored 3/10). So health gets the first and biggest push here.

**The first tasks, in order:**
1. **Boil drinking water**, or make tea from boiled water, at the court and the port. Cheap, and it stops most gut disease.
2. **Oral rehydration drink**: a pinch of salt and a fist of palm sugar in a liter of boiled water. Teach every midwife and mother. This is the biggest life-saver in a hot country.
3. **Latrines away from wells and rice fields**, and **shoes or sandals** in the fields (against hookworm).
4. **Drain standing water** and keep the edges of rice fields and ponds free of weeds. **Fish ponds stocked with small fish** eat mosquito larvae.
5. **Mosquito nets** of fine cotton, first for children and new mothers.
6. **Wormwood (qinghao)** for malaria, grown from seed bought from Chinese traders. Steeped in cold water, as the old Chinese recipe says. *(It works best as a modern extract. A simple brew may help only partly. Mark this as uncertain.)*
7. **Keep the rice bran.** Polished white rice causes **beriberi**. He pushes **parboiled or lightly milled rice**.
8. **Midwife school:** clean hands, boiled cloths, cut the cord with a boiled blade.
9. **Quarantine for ships** coming from places with outbreaks: 10 days on an island near the port.
10. **Cholera readiness:** cholera's home is Bengal, a trading partner. *(Whether it reached Java in the 1200s is uncertain.)* Clean water and rehydration are the defense either way.

**Food tasks:**
- **Row planting, spacing and field drying** for rice.
- **Seed selection:** keep seed from the best plants each harvest.
- **Ducks and fish in rice fields.**
- **Grain stores** in every district, kept dry and raised against rats, holding **one year of rice** by 1256.
- **New crops from trade:** more sugarcane, bananas, citrus, soybeans and mung beans from China, sorghum from India as a drought crop.

**Water:**
- **Dams and canals** on the Brantas and its streams (Java already has irrigation; he adds surveying with a water level and a better layout).
- **Flood control** on the Brantas, which floods and carries volcanic mud.

## Trade and the outside world
**The gate:** **one main port** on the Brantas mouth (Hujung Galuh, later also **Tuban** and **Gresik** on the north coast). All foreign trade goes through it under one set of written rules.

**What goes out:**
- **Spices** (cloves from Ternate and Tidore, nutmeg and mace from Banda, pepper), carried in his ships
- **Rice** (Java's surplus feeds the trading ports of the whole region)
- **Better ships**, sold to friends, and later **iron tools**
- **Cotton cloth and batik**, **sandalwood** from Timor, **tin** from Bangka

**What comes in:**
- **Iron** (Luwu, then China), **copper**, silk and porcelain from China
- **Cloth and glass** from India
- **Crops and seeds** from everywhere
- **People:** craftsmen, scholars, refugees
- **News** from every captain: who is at war, where prices are high

**The spice idea:** instead of conquering the Spice Islands, he **partners with the sultans and chiefs** of Ternate, Tidore and Banda. They grow, he ships and pays fairly. His ships are faster and safer, so they win the trade without force. *(The Ternate and Tidore sultanates formally date from the later 1200s; before that they're chiefdoms.)*

**Keeping secrets, or letting them go:**
- **Let spread on purpose:** rice methods, boiled water, rehydration, latrines. Health in neighbors means fewer epidemics arriving by ship.
- **Keep close:** the **longitude method**, the **charts**, the **clock**, the **steam engine** and the **location of the Australian iron**. Taught only at the navigation school, to sworn masters.
- **Can't be kept:** ship design. Chinese and Indian traders will see the hulls. He accepts this and stays **one generation ahead** instead.

**China:** he keeps Java **a useful trading partner** to the Song, then Kublai's Yuan, with polite embassies and gifts.

## Story texture
**Tensions:**
- **Everyone can see his work.** In a port full of foreign captains, every new hull is copied. He has to decide what to give away and what to hide.
- **A court of knives.** The kingdom was founded on a murder, and the next two kings are murdered. He's a teacher in a palace where teaching the wrong prince gets you killed.
- **Sea vs land.** The old Javanese nobility think of power as rice land and temples. He thinks of power as ships and ports. The court has to be won over to the sea.
- **Two homes.** Java is crowded and rich; Australia is empty, dry and full of iron. Who will go, and how are the **Aboriginal peoples** of the north treated fairly?
- **The quiet eruption.** He knows Samalas is coming in 1257. He can't say so. He fills the granaries for 15 years under excuses, and people think he's a miser until the sky goes dark.

**Real people he'd meet:**
- **Anusapati**, the fearful king.
- **Ranggawuni (Wisnuwardhana)** and **Mahisa Cempaka**, the princes he protects.
- **Kertanegara**, the brilliant, proud student, the one who in real history insulted Kublai's envoy.
- **Raden Wijaya**, the young noble who in real history founded Majapahit. In this variation, maybe the hero's best navigator.
- **Meng Qi**, Kublai's envoy, in 1289.
- **Marco Polo**, who sailed home through these seas in **1292** and wrote about Java's wealth.

**Scenes:**
- The first time a captain finds a far island **exactly where the hero's chart said it would be**.
- The **eclipse** night: he and Ranggawuni alone on a temple roof, waiting.
- Smuggling the princes out of the palace in **1248**.
- The **sky going dark in 1257**, and the granaries opening while neighbors starve.
- Meng Qi arriving in **1289**, and Kertanegara choosing **not** to scar him because of his old tutor.
- The first ship to reach the **red iron hills of the Pilbara**, and a test furnace on the beach.
- A Javanese steamship crossing to Australia in four days instead of three weeks.

## Open questions
1. **How much to hide?** Should he share navigation openly (more trade for everyone) or keep it as the realm's secret edge?
2. **Avoid the 1293 fleet, or let it come?** Calming Kublai in 1289 may stop it, but that changes history a lot (no Majapahit as we know it).
3. **What role for Raden Wijaya** (the real founder of Majapahit)? Student, rival, heir?
