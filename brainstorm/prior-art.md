# Has this been done? (Yes — it's a whole genre)

The genre goes by "**time-travel uplift**", "**Connecticut Yankee**" stories, or "**isekai kingdom-building**" (Japanese/Chinese web novels). Being well-trodden is fine: the question is what our version does differently.

## Classic novels
- **A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court** — Mark Twain, 1889. A factory boss lands in Arthur's England, uses an eclipse prediction to gain power, builds telegraphs, newspapers, guns and schools. Ends darkly: the Church turns the people against him and his tech army massacres knights. Satire of "progress".
- **Lest Darkness Fall** — L. Sprague de Camp, 1939. The big one. An archaeologist drops into 535 AD Rome and tries to stop the Dark Ages. Starts with **distilled brandy, double-entry bookkeeping and Arabic numerals**, then printing, newspapers, semaphore telegraph. Very realistic: most of his big ideas fail because the craftsmen can't make them.
- **The Cross-Time Engineer (Adventures of Conrad Stargard)** — Leo Frankowski, 1986+. **Closest to Tim's idea.** A Polish engineer lands in 1231 Poland, knows the Mongols invade in 1241, and industrializes as fast as possible to stop them: mills, steel, railways, rifles. Mixed reviews (dated attitudes), but the premise is nearly identical.
- **1632 / Ring of Fire series** — Eric Flint, 2000+. A whole modern West Virginia town lands in the Thirty Years' War. Focuses on *what you can maintain once modern stuff breaks* — very grounded.
- **Island in the Sea of Time** — S.M. Stirling, 1998. Nantucket island goes to 1250 BC Bronze Age.
- **Rome, Sweet Rome** — a 2011 Reddit story: a modern U.S. Marine battalion in Augustus' Rome. Optioned for a film.

## Manga / anime / web novels (huge in Asia)
- **Dr. Stone** — Senku rebuilds civilization from the stone age: soap, glass, antibiotics, electricity, radio, a cell phone. Shows each tech's *prerequisites* step by step. Great reference for "what do you need first".
- **Release That Witch** (Chinese web novel) — engineer reincarnated as a medieval prince, steam engines and artillery.
- **How a Realist Hero Rebuilt the Kingdom** — modern guy made king, focuses on economics, logistics and politics.
- **Ascendance of a Bookworm** — a girl reinvents paper and printing from scratch.
- **Pharmacist / medicine isekai** (Parallel World Pharmacy) — modern medicine in a medieval world.

## Non-fiction (research gold for us)
- **How to Invent Everything** — Ryan North, 2018. Written as a guide for a stranded time traveler. Literally our research book.
- **The Knowledge: How to Rebuild Civilization in the Aftermath of a Cataclysm** — Lewis Dartnell, 2014.

## What's been done to death vs. open space
- Done a lot: guns and cannons win wars; the hero is a lone genius; the Church as villain.
- **Less explored (possible angles for us):**
  - The dystopian-future framing: the traveler was *trained and sent on purpose*, carrying a memorized "codex". Explains why he knows so much.
  - Focus on **simple ideas with massive effects** (Tim's favorite) instead of weapons.
  - The cost of knowing too much: he can predict the future but every change makes his knowledge less reliable.
  - An EE-flavored hero: electricity and communications as the edge, not just steel and guns.
