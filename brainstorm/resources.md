# Resources — where the wealth is, and who got rich

Tim (2026-10-27): "specific locations where natural resources would be extremely significant ... iron/coal/silver/gold mines and mountains where the people who discovered it and manufactured it grew rich. Also things like spice, foods, etc."

All of these are on the website's **Map** tab. "Found" = when it was really discovered or became big. A time traveler can show up **before** that and say "dig here."

## Silver (the money metal of the 1200s–1700s)
| Place | Found | Who got rich |
|---|---|---|
| **Rammelsberg**, Goslar (Germany) | 968 | Holy Roman emperors |
| **Freiberg**, Saxony | 1168 | Saxony |
| **Kutná Hora**, Bohemia | ~1290s | Bohemia's kings; their coin spread across Europe |
| **Schwaz**, Tyrol | 1400s | The **Fugger** bankers, richest family in Europe |
| **Joachimsthal**, Bohemia | 1516 | Its coin, the "thaler", is where the word **dollar** comes from |
| **Potosí**, Bolivia | 1545 | The Spanish Empire. A "silver mountain"; "worth a Potosí" meant priceless |
| **Zacatecas & Guanajuato**, Mexico | 1546+ | Spain; Mexico became the world's top silver producer |
| **Iwami Ginzan**, Japan | 1526 | Japan, then ~1/3 of the world's silver |

## Gold
| Place | Found | Who got rich |
|---|---|---|
| **Bambuk, Bure & Akan fields**, West Africa | Ancient | Mali Empire. **Mansa Musa** gave away so much gold on his 1324 pilgrimage that Cairo's gold price crashed for years |
| **Mapungubwe & Great Zimbabwe**, southern Africa | ~1000s–1200s | Their kings, through Indian Ocean trade |
| **Witwatersrand**, South Africa | 1886 | ~40% of all gold ever mined. Deep and hard to mine |
| **Minas Gerais**, Brazil | 1690s | Portugal |
| **California** | 1848 | Loose gold in rivers: anyone with a pan |
| **Victoria**, Australia | 1851 | Huge surface nuggets; Melbourne became one of the richest cities on Earth |
| **Otago** NZ, **Klondike** Canada, **Kalgoorlie** Australia | 1860s–1890s | Gold rushes |

**Story note:** the **easy** gold is the **loose river gold** of California, Victoria, Otago and the Klondike: no deep mines needed, just knowing where to look.

## Iron and coal (the industrial pair — best when close together)
| Place | Has | Story value |
|---|---|---|
| **Newcastle** & **South Wales** (Britain) | Coal by the sea + iron | The real Industrial Revolution. Newcastle already ships "sea coal" in the 1200s |
| **Liège** (Belgium) | Coal + iron | The first place on the continent to industrialize |
| **Ruhr** (Germany), **Silesia** (Poland) | Coal + iron | Germany's industrial heart |
| **Bergslagen & Kiruna** (Sweden) | The purest iron ore | Swedish iron was famous across Europe |
| **Bilbao** (Spain), **Elba** (Italy), **Erzberg** (Austria), **Lorraine** (France) | Iron ore | Mined since Roman times |
| **Pittsburgh** (USA) | Coal + iron + rivers | "Steel City", Carnegie |

| **Shanxi / Henan** (China) | Coal + iron | Song China already makes iron with coal in the 1000s |

| **Hunter Valley** (Australia) | Coal right at the coast | Sydney's industry |

## Copper and tin (bronze, wire, electricity)
- **Falun** (Sweden): the "Great Copper Mountain" paid for Sweden's 1600s empire.
- **Rio Tinto** (Spain): mined since the Phoenicians; still a mining giant's name.
- **Lake Superior's Keweenaw**: **pure metallic copper** lying in the rock; Native Americans hammered it for thousands of years. Perfect for an electricity-focused hero.
- **Chuquicamata** (Chile), **Katanga** (Africa's copperbelt), **Phalaborwa** (South Africa), **Butte** (USA).
- **Tin:** **Cornwall** (England), the **Erzgebirge** (Germany/Bohemia), **Bangka** (Indonesia) and **Malaya**, **Bolivia**, **Rooiberg** (South Africa).

## Chemistry resources
| Resource | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Saltpeter** | Bihar (India), China, **Atacama nitrates** (Chile) | Gunpowder and fertilizer. Chile, Peru and Bolivia fought a war over the Atacama (1879) |
| **Guano** | Chincha Islands (Peru) | Bird droppings piled 50 m deep. The richest fertilizer on Earth; made Peru rich in the 1840s–60s |
| **Sulfur** | Sicily, volcanoes (Java, Japan, Iceland) | Sulfuric acid and gunpowder |
| **Mercury** | Almadén (Spain), Huancavelica (Peru), Idrija (Slovenia) | Needed to refine silver |
| **Salt** | **Wieliczka** (Poland), Hallstatt (Austria), Lüneburg (Germany), Taghaza (Sahara), **Zigong** (China) | Preserving food; taxes. Wieliczka paid ~1/3 of Poland's royal income. Zigong drilled brine wells **1,000 m deep with bamboo** |
| **Oil & bitumen** | **Baku**, Pennsylvania, Trinidad's Pitch Lake, Burma, Persia | Lamps, lubricants, later engines. Baku and Burma already have oil seeps and hand-dug wells |
| **Kaolin clay** | Jingdezhen (China), Cornwall | Porcelain |

## Spices (the most profitable goods in the world)
| Spice | Where (only place!) | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Nutmeg & mace** | **Banda Islands** (Indonesia), tiny | Marked up ~60,000% by the time it reached Europe. The Dutch traded away **Manhattan** to keep one Banda island (1667) |
| **Cloves** | **Ternate & Tidore** (Maluku) | Same story |
| **Black pepper** | **Malabar coast**, India | "Black gold"; sometimes used as money |
| **Cinnamon** | **Sri Lanka** | Its source was hidden by Arab traders' myths for centuries |

## Foods, crops and goods
- **Sugar** (India → the Mediterranean → the Caribbean): "white gold". (Historically built on slavery.)
- **Silk** and **porcelain** (China), **cotton cloth** (Gujarat & Bengal, India), **tea** (China), **coffee** (Ethiopia, then Mocha in Yemen).
- From the Americas: **potato, maize, cacao** (used as money by the Aztecs), **vanilla, tobacco, rubber, quinine**, and **cochineal**, a red dye from insects that was Spain's second-richest export after silver.
- **Fish:** North Sea and Baltic **herring** (the Dutch "built Amsterdam on herring bones"), **cod** of the Grand Banks.
- **Wool:** England (paid for medieval England), Castile's guarded **merino sheep**.
- **Diamonds:** Golconda (India, the only source until 1725), later Kimberley (South Africa).
- **Furs** (Russia, Canada), **amber** (Baltic), **ivory** (Africa).

## Shipping chokepoints (who controls these controls trade)
**Strait of Malacca** (Asia's main sea road), **Hormuz** (Persian Gulf), **Bab-el-Mandeb** (Red Sea), **Gibraltar**, **Bosporus**, **the Øresund** (Denmark charged a toll on every ship for 400 years), and later the **Cape of Good Hope**, **Suez** and **Panama**.
