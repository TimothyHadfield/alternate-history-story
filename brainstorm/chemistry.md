# Chemistry — "hard to know, easy to make"

Tim (2026-10-27): "chemical processes or developments of molecules might be really good to do because they're so difficult to find and know what to do with, but actually making them might be very simple mixing of ingredients, heating/pressure, etc."

He's right. Chemistry is mostly **recipes**: what to mix, in what ratio, at what heat. The discovery took centuries of trial and error, but following the recipe often needs only pots, fire and patience. A traveler with recipes skips the centuries.

Difficulty: 🟢 = village workshop · 🟡 = needs Tier 1 chemistry (acids, glassware, good furnaces) · 🔴 = needs an industrial base.

## The "mother chemicals" (make these first; nearly everything else needs them)
| Chemical | Recipe (simplified) | Unlocks | Diff. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lye** (potash/caustic) | Soak wood ash in water, boil down | Soap, glass, bleaching | 🟢 |
| **Quicklime / slaked lime** | Bake limestone in a kiln | Mortar, concrete, farming, tanning, water treatment | 🟢 |
| **Sulfuric acid** ("oil of vitriol") | Burn sulfur + a little saltpeter over water in a lead-lined room (lead chamber process, 1746) | The single most important industrial chemical: other acids, fertilizer, ether, batteries, dyes | 🟡 |
| **Nitric acid** | Saltpeter + sulfuric acid, distilled | Explosives, dyes, etching, silver refining | 🟡 |
| **Hydrochloric acid** | Salt + sulfuric acid | Cleaning metals, chlorine, gelatin | 🟡 |
| **Soda ash** (Leblanc process, 1791) | Salt + sulfuric acid → then roast with limestone + coal | Cheap glass and soap at scale | 🟡 |
| **Alcohol** | Distill wine or beer | Antiseptic, solvent, fuel, ether | 🟢 |

## Gunpowder ⭐
- **Recipe:** 75% saltpeter, 15% charcoal, 10% sulfur.
- **The hard part is saltpeter.** Make it in "nitre beds": heaps of manure, urine, straw and wood ash, left to rot for about a year, then leached with water and crystallized. Every farm is a potential gunpowder factory.
- **The secret trick: "corning."** Wet the mix, press it into cakes and break them into grains. Grained powder burns far faster and more evenly than loose powder. This alone made cannons truly effective in the 1400s.
- In a 1230s Europe, gunpowder is only a rumor from China. He'd have it decades early, and understand it far better.

## Pulling nitrogen from the air (feeding everyone) ⭐
Plants are starved for **nitrogen**. Air is 78% nitrogen, but it's locked up tight. Ways to get it, from easiest to hardest:
1. 🟢 **Legumes** (clover, beans, peas). Bacteria in their roots fix nitrogen for free. Crop rotation with clover is the zero-tech version.
2. 🟢 **Manure, urine, guano, saltpeter.** Collected nitrogen.
3. 🟡 **Superphosphate** (Lawes, 1842): bones + sulfuric acid. Not nitrogen, but phosphorus is the other big missing nutrient, and this is absurdly simple. **Pair it with clover and yields jump.**
4. 🔴⚡ **The arc process** (Birkeland–Eyde, 1903): blast air with a big electric arc. Lightning-style heat makes nitrogen and oxygen combine; the gas is bubbled into water to make nitric acid → fertilizer *and* explosives. **Simple concept, but it eats electricity.** Perfect for an EE hero with a hydropower dam. Strong story candidate.
5. 🔴 **Haber-Bosch** (1909–1913): nitrogen + hydrogen → ammonia, over an iron catalyst at ~450°C and ~200 times air pressure. **This is the exception to "easy to make"**: the recipe is simple but the pressure needs thick steel vessels, good seals and compressors. Fritz Haber won the chemistry; Carl Bosch spent years on the engineering. Endgame tech. (Today it feeds about half of humanity.)
   - *Story note:* Haber also invented chemical warfare. Same chemistry feeds people and kills them. Good theme.

## Medicine and health
| | Recipe / source | Diff. |
|---|---|---|
| **Soap** | Lye + fat, boiled | 🟢 |
| **Chlorine bleach** | Salt water + electricity (electrolysis), or HCl + manganese ore | 🟡 |
| **Water disinfection** | A little bleach in drinking water; or **SODIS**: clear bottles in full sun for 6 hours (UV kills germs) | 🟢 |
| **Iodine tincture** | Seaweed ash + sulfuric acid → iodine, dissolve in alcohol | 🟡 |
| **Ether (anesthesia)** ⭐ | Alcohol + sulfuric acid, gently heated, distilled. Painless surgery 600 years early | 🟡 |
| **Willow bark → aspirin** | Willow bark tea works now; true aspirin needs acetic anhydride | 🟢 / 🔴 |
| **Penicillin** | Grow mold on bread/melon, extract. Easy to grow, hard to concentrate in big doses | 🟡→🔴 |
| **Quinine** (malaria) | Cinchona bark — South America only | 🟢 if reachable |

## Explosives, fire and light
| | Recipe | Diff. |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitroglycerin → dynamite** | Glycerin (a soap-making byproduct!) + nitric + sulfuric acid, kept cold. Soak into diatomaceous earth = dynamite (Nobel, 1867). Mining and canals become 10× faster. Very dangerous to make | 🟡 |
| **Guncotton** | Cotton + nitric + sulfuric acid → smokeless powder | 🟡 |
| **Matches** | Phosphorus (from boiled-down urine or bone ash) + potassium chlorate | 🟡 |
| **Coal gas lighting** | Heat coal in a sealed iron pot, pipe the gas. Byproduct: coal tar (dyes, medicines later) and **coke** (for steel) | 🟡 |
| **Acetylene torch** | Calcium carbide + water → acetylene. Burn with oxygen to cut and weld steel | 🔴 (carbide needs an electric furnace) |
| **Thermite** | Aluminum powder + rust. Welds rails. Needs cheap aluminum | 🔴 |

## Building materials
- 🟢 **Roman concrete** (lime + volcanic ash), 🟡 **Portland cement** (limestone + clay at ~1450°C).
- 🟡 **Clear glass** (pure sand + soda ash + lime; lead oxide for optical "crystal").
- 🟢 **Photography** (1830s tech!): silver nitrate (silver + nitric acid) on paper + salt, exposed in a camera box, fixed with salt water or "hypo". Maps, records, spying, propaganda.
- 🔴 **Vulcanized rubber** (rubber + sulfur, heated) — needs tropical rubber trees.
- 🔴 **Aluminum** (electrolysis of alumina in molten cryolite) — needs big electricity. Was once worth more than gold.

## Story ideas from this file
- The **"alchemist" who actually knows**: people think he's a sorcerer, but he's just following recipes.
- **Saltpeter becomes the strategic resource.** Nitre-bed farms, saltpeter wars, guarding urine (real history: Britain and Prussia did this).
- **Dual use:** the same nitric acid that fertilizes fields makes explosives.
