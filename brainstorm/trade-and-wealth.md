# Trade and wealth — what makes a small society rich

Tim (2026-10-27): "think about each invention or process that might allow that specific society to trade with other nations and grow extremely rich ... Shipping might be something that could be very important."

On the website, the **💰 Trade** button on the Ideas tab shows every invention with export value.

## The golden rules
1. **Sell products, never processes.** Export the glass, not the furnace recipe.
2. **Sell luxuries first.** Small, light, very valuable goods (mirrors, spirits, dyes, spectacles) earn the most per ship and attract the least attention.
3. **Never sell weapons or machine tools.** Gunpowder, rifles, telescopes for war and lathes stay home.
4. **Use middlemen early** so buyers don't know where the goods come from.
5. **Turn gold and silver into *capability*.** Buy skilled people, animals, seeds, rare metals and books, not palaces.

## What to sell, by stage
### Stage 1 (years 1–10): simple chemistry and craft, huge markups
| Product | Why it sells | Real proof |
|---|---|---|
| **Distilled spirits** (brandy, rum) | Doesn't spoil, light, everyone wants it | Became a global currency in the 1600s–1700s |
| **Soap** (fine, scented) | A luxury in most places | Aleppo and Castile soap were famous |
| **Clear glass & silvered mirrors** | Rare and dazzling | Venice's mirrors cost more than paintings |
| **Spectacles** | Old craftsmen and scholars keep working | Invented ~1286; sold all over Europe |
| **Dyes** (Prussian blue, cochineal-like reds) | Colored cloth = status. Blue and red were the priciest colors | Prussian blue (1706) is an easy recipe: potash + dried blood + iron salts |
| **Refined white sugar** | "White gold" | Needs sugarcane (tropics) or sugar beets (later) |
| **Paper & printed books** | Books cost as much as a house | Gutenberg's press cut book costs ~90% |
| **Preserved food** (canned, salted, distilled) | Navies and armies pay well | |

### Stage 2 (years 10–40): better materials
- **Steel blades and tools** (crucible steel): Damascus steel was legendary.
- **Porcelain**: China kept it secret ~1,000 years and Europe paid in silver; Meissen cracked it in 1708 with kaolin and a very hot kiln.
- **Clocks and navigation instruments**, **maps** (accurate maps are military secrets *and* trade goods).
- **Medicines**: ether, quinine, iodine, soap and the **knowledge of oral rehydration** (sell cures, not formulas).
- **Fertilizer** (superphosphate), **cement**, **paper**.
- **Trusted money**: machine-struck coins with **ridged edges** (they can't be clipped). If your coin is the most trusted, you become the **bank** of the region.
- **Insurance and banking**: the Italians and Dutch got rich insuring other people's ships.

### Stage 3 (years 40–100): industry
- **Machine-made cloth**: the #1 export of the real Industrial Revolution. Britain's mills clothed the world.
- **Rails, iron, steel**, **kerosene for lamps** (Standard Oil got rich selling light), **matches**, **dynamite** for other people's mines.
- **Services**: shipping, telegraph cables, insurance, engineering.

## Shipping: get rich carrying other people's goods
- **The Dutch fluyt** (1590s): a cheap, roomy cargo ship that needed half the crew of rivals. It let the Dutch carry most of Europe's trade. Simple design idea, enormous effect.
- **Copper-bottomed hulls** (no shipworms, faster), **better rigging**, **accurate navigation** and **chronometers** (fewer wrecks = lower insurance).
- **Steamships** ignore the wind: whoever has them first owns the sea lanes.
- **Own a chokepoint.** Denmark taxed every ship through the Øresund for 400 years. Malacca, Hormuz and later the **Cape of Good Hope** are the big ones.
- **Ports and warehouses**: whoever runs the market town takes a cut of everything.

## Raw resources worth more with tech
Knowledge turns "useless" ground into wealth: **guano and nitrate deserts** (fertilizer), **sulfur volcanoes** (acid), **oil seeps** (lamp fuel), **pure copper** (wire), **bauxite** (aluminum, later), **kaolin clay** (porcelain).
