# How long each idea takes

Tim (2026-10-27): "could we categorize each thing (either invention or procedure) by how long it would take to actually develop or implement? ... (these aren't actual numbers, but you get the point?)"

The full list with numbers lives in [data/ideas.json](../data/ideas.json) and shows on the website's **Ideas** tab, grouped by time.

## What the time means
**From the day the hero decides to do it, to the day it works at a useful scale** — not until the whole country uses it.

Assumptions:
- He already has power, money and a workshop with good local craftsmen.
- Setting is roughly 1230s Europe.
- All of its prerequisites already exist. Times **stack**: the steam engine's 8 years start after the blast furnace is done.
- These are story estimates, not research numbers. Change any of them freely.

## Time groups
| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Instant – 2 months** | Oral rehydration, quarantine, codes, eclipse predictions, soap |
| **3 – 11 months** | Handwashing habit, bookkeeping, three-plate flatness, delousing |
| **1 – 2 years** | Latrines, cowpox vaccine, printing press, gunpowder, telescope, semaphore |
| **3 – 5 years** | Crop rotation results, sulfuric acid, ether, lathe, blast furnace |
| **6 – 10 years** | Newcomen engine, electric telegraph, penicillin, public schools |
| **11 – 20 years** | Watt engine, Bessemer steel, locomotives, railways, radio |
| **20+ years** | Arc-process nitrogen, aluminum, Haber-Bosch |

## The big takeaway
Almost every **health, math and organization** idea finishes in the first two years, and those are what make him powerful and trusted. Industry takes **15–20 years** of stacked steps. So the story has a natural shape: **years 1–2 = miracles, years 3–10 = foundations, years 10–25 = industrial revolution.**
