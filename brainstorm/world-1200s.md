# The world around 1230 — what people knew and had

Tim (2026-10-27): "could you also talk about what the people in the 1200s know as well as what kind of technology they had"

This is the starting line. Everything the hero builds is measured against it. Location isn't chosen yet, so this covers the whole world. Each idea on the website's **Ideas** tab also has a short "In 1230" line.

## Big picture
- **About 400 million people on Earth.** Europe ~70 million, China ~100 million+, India ~80 million.
- **The most advanced places are NOT in Europe.** Song China and the Islamic world (Cairo, Damascus, Baghdad, Spain) lead in science, medicine, industry and cities.
- **Biggest cities:** Hangzhou (China, ~1 million), Cairo, Constantinople, Baghdad. Paris is ~100–200k; London ~50k.
- **The Mongols are rising.** Genghis Khan died in 1227. His sons are conquering China, Persia and Russia. It's the biggest event of the century.
- Most people everywhere are **farmers**, most can't read, and few people travel more than a day's walk from home.

## Who was ahead at what
| Region | Strengths in 1230 |
|---|---|
| **Song China** | Gunpowder weapons, wood-block printing, paper money, the compass, cast iron in huge amounts, coal, big ships with watertight compartments, seed drills, early-ripening rice, civil service exams |
| **Islamic world** | Algebra, trigonometry, Arabic numerals, optics (Alhazen), chemistry (distillation, acids in small amounts), the best hospitals on Earth, paper mills, fine glass and soap, astronomy (astrolabes) |
| **India** | Place-value numbers and zero (they invented them), advanced math with early pieces of calculus, wootz crucible steel, cotton textiles, zinc smelting |
| **Europe** | Watermills and windmills everywhere, heavy plows, Gothic cathedrals (excellent masons), new universities, Italian banking and trade, castles and armored knights |
| **Korea / Japan** | Korea casts metal movable type (1234); Japan has superb sword steel |
| **Africa** | Mali Empire rising (gold trade); Great Zimbabwe; Ethiopian stone churches |
| **Americas** | Cahokia, Maya cities, the Andes. Completely unknown to the rest of the world |

## What people KNEW (and believed)
### Math
- **Known:** Euclid's geometry, arithmetic, algebra (al-Khwarizmi), trigonometry (Islamic and Indian), the abacus.
- **In Europe:** Roman numerals are still normal. Fibonacci's book (1202) is just introducing Arabic numerals to Italian merchants.
- **Unknown:** logarithms, calculus (beyond Indian pieces), statistics, probability.

### Sky and Earth
- **Known:** the Earth is round (every educated person knows; the flat-earth myth is modern). Its rough size was measured by the Greeks. Astronomers predict eclipses roughly. The astrolabe measures star angles.
- **Believed:** the Earth is the center and the planets ride crystal spheres (Ptolemy). Comets are omens. Astrology is part of medicine and politics.
- **Maps:** decent for the Mediterranean, China and the Indian Ocean. Al-Idrisi's 1154 world map is the best. Nobody knows the Americas, Australia or Antarctica. Africa's southern shape is unknown.

### Body and medicine
- **Believed:** disease comes from imbalanced "four humors" (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile) or "bad air" (miasma). Treatment = bloodletting, purging, herbs, prayer.
- **Known:** good anatomy basics from Galen (partly wrong), herbal drugs, setting bones, some surgery. Islamic hospitals keep records and train doctors. Salerno and Montpellier have medical schools.
- **Unknown:** germs, vaccines, vitamins, blood circulation, how diseases spread.
- **Life:** average life expectancy ~30–35, mostly because 30–50% of children die young. Adults who survive childhood often live to 50–60.

### Matter and physics
- **Believed:** everything is made of four elements (earth, water, air, fire). Alchemists try to turn lead into gold. Heavy things fall faster (Aristotle). A vacuum is impossible.
- **Known:** 7 metals (gold, silver, copper, iron, tin, lead, mercury; India also has zinc), sulfur, salts, alum, vitriol. Lenses magnify. Lodestones point north.

## What TECHNOLOGY they had
| Area | What exists |
|---|---|
| **Farming** | Heavy plow, horse collar, horseshoes, three-field rotation (Europe), irrigation, terraces, early-ripening rice (China) |
| **Power** | Waterwheels (England alone had 5,600+ mills by 1086), windmills (new in Europe), animals, human muscle. No engines |
| **Metals** | Iron and steel by smiths; charcoal blast furnaces (China huge, Europe early); crucible steel (India, Persia); bronze bell casting; gold and silver work |
| **Building** | Gothic cathedrals with flying buttresses, stone castles, lime mortar (Roman concrete recipe is lost), cranes on treadwheels |
| **Machines** | Pulleys, gears, cranks, screw presses (wine, oil), pole lathes, water-powered hammers and bellows, Al-Jazari's automatons and water clocks (1206) |
| **Glass** | Stained glass, Venetian and Syrian fine glass, reading stones. No clear window glass for ordinary homes |
| **Chemistry** | Distillation (small), dyes, soap, tanning, salt, lime, alchemists' acids in tiny amounts. Gunpowder in China only |
| **Writing** | Parchment in Europe; paper in China, the Islamic world and Spain. Books are hand-copied and cost as much as a house. China and Korea print |
| **Time** | Sundials, water clocks, candles, church bells. Mechanical clocks come ~1280s |
| **Travel** | Horses, carts on mud roads, river boats. A message across a kingdom takes weeks |
| **Ships** | Cogs and galleys in Europe; the sternpost rudder and compass are new. Chinese junks and Arab dhows sail the Indian Ocean |
| **War** | Swords, spears, chain mail, crossbows, horse archers (Mongols), counterweight trebuchets, stone castles. Chinese bombs and fire lances |
| **Money** | Silver coins, bills of exchange, Italian banks, trade fairs; paper money in China |

## The gap (where the hero's ideas land)
Things nobody on Earth has in 1230: germ theory, vaccines, oral rehydration, the scientific method as a system, standard measures, calculus, logarithms, statistics, clocks with pendulums, telescopes, microscopes, thermometers, precision machining, steam power, electricity, chemistry beyond recipes, knowledge of the Americas.

Things that exist *somewhere* but not everywhere (he could import or copy instead of invent): **Arabic numerals, paper, printing, gunpowder, the compass, crucible steel, the seed drill, good hospitals.** A hero in Europe could gain a lot just by bringing the best of China and the Islamic world home.
