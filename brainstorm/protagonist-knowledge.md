# What the traveler has to know

The core problem of every uplift story: **knowing *that* something works is not knowing *how* to make it.** Most of us know steam pushes a piston; few know the cylinder tolerance, the gasket material, or how to make the iron.

## Knowledge areas (roughly in order of payoff)
1. **Health & sanitation** — germ theory, oral rehydration, vaccination, wound care, childbirth. Fast, visible wins → loyalty and power.
2. **Agriculture** — crop rotation, legumes, fertilizers, tools. More food is the base of everything.
3. **Math & bookkeeping** — numerals, algebra, statistics, double-entry. Makes the state and treasury efficient.
4. **Geology & geography** — where the coal, iron, copper, tin, salt, sulfur and gold are; where the trade routes and the New World are. *Knowing where to dig is worth an army.*
5. **Metallurgy** — furnaces, coke, steel, alloys (bronze, brass).
6. **Chemistry** — acids, bases, soap, gunpowder, distillation, ether. Recipes matter more than theory.
7. **Mechanics & machining** — lathes, screws, gears, standard measures, steam engines.
8. **Electricity** — batteries, magnets, wire, telegraph, generators.
9. **Physics & astronomy** — Newton's laws, optics, orbits (calculus), navigation, eclipse prediction (a classic way to look like a prophet).
10. **History & people** — who rules where, upcoming wars, plagues, famines; how to persuade a king, run a court, avoid being burned as a heretic.

## Who could know all that? (ideas)
- **A regular engineering student** (like Tim) — relatable; knows physics, electricity and math cold; has to rediscover chemistry and metallurgy by trial and error. Lots of failure = good drama.
- **A trained agent from the dystopian future** — chosen and drilled for years, memorized a "codex" of key recipes. Explains the breadth. Risk: too perfect.
- **A mix:** an ordinary person given a crash course (or a damaged/partial data device) before being sent. He knows *some* things well, *some* things vaguely, and has gaps that create problems.

## Open questions
- Does he bring anything physical (a book, a tablet with dying battery, a few tools)?
- How much does he remember precisely vs. vaguely?
- How does he gain power in the first place?
